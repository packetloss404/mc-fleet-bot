# SubTropolis — Development Plan (Longer-Term Evolution)

> **Architectural Designer deliverable for the human project lead and downstream phases.**
> Companion to `design-plan.md` and `working-plan.md`. This document specifies **how the build evolves over time** — the MVP, the phased delivery versions, the future extensions, and the open items for the user.
> The Site Planner's `site-plan.md` and `site-coordinates.json` are upstream dependencies for the site placement, but not for the MVP cut.

---

## 1. MVP Definition

The minimum viable build (MVP) is the smallest version of SubTropolis that **captures the soul** of the place — utilitarian awe, the boring grandeur, the 1960s industrial warehouse under a hill.

### 1.1 The non-negotiable must-haves (in the MVP)

- **The horizontal portal in a ravine wall** (`[D]`). The entrance is the *only* thing that distinguishes SubTropolis from Louisville Mega Cavern and from every other mine-turned-warehouse. Without it, the build is a generic underground warehouse.
- **The pillar grid** (`[D]`). The 8×8 white concrete pillars on the iconic-intersection grid are the *single most iconic image* of SubTropolis. Without the pillar grid, the build has no signature.
- **The main avenue with painted pillar numbers + 15 mph speed limit + at least 3 street signs (Hushpuckney + 2 invented companions)** (`[D]` + `[X]`). The signage is what makes the place *read* as SubTropolis and not as a generic Minecraft cave.
- **At least 3 tenant fit-outs** (`[D]` for tenant identity, `[I]` for layout). USPS, NARA, and STC are the three most-documented and most-iconic tenants; they cover the three primary archetypes (package logistics, records archive, data center).
- **The "no weather" feel** (`[D]`). The chamber is fully sealed, no sky, no mobs in the deep (a fun optional rule for survival servers).

### 1.2 What gets cut from the MVP and can come back later

- **The surface building and parking lot**: a single Hunt Midwest sign at the portal mouth is enough; the full visitor center and parking lot are nice-to-have.
- **The exit portal (second portal)**: a dark archway at the end of the main avenue is enough; the full perpendicular-face exit on the ravine is a v1.0+ feature.
- **The inter-site connections (service tunnel, public shaft)**: these are project fictions for the combined-complex project; they can be added in v1.5+.
- **The ghost mine chamber**: a *single* dark side spur with a barricade and a "DANGER — ACTIVE MINING" sign is the minimum; the full chamber with the minecart prop and the deepslate veining is a v1.0+ feature.
- **The Hunt Hall round room**: a single Lamar Hunt plaque on a pillar at the central plaza is enough for the MVP; the round room is a v1.0+ feature.
- **The employee cafeteria, shift-change signage, Groundhog Run banner**: these are atmospheric easter eggs; the MVP can ship without them.
- **The four minor fit-out archetypes (Grainger, EPA, Hallmark/Russell Stover, UV&S, Ford/Grainger historical)**: these can be added in v1.0+; the MVP is the three marquee tenants.

### 1.3 What the MVP feels like

A first-time visitor to the MVP walks into a ravine wall, descends a gentle ramp, and emerges into a vast painted-pillar grid. The first intersection is visible from the bottom of the ramp — a 3×3 grid of 8×8 white concrete pillars vanishing into the distance under sea-lantern ceiling strips. The visitor walks the main avenue, passes a "Hushpuckney Ave" street sign, sees a "SPEED LIMIT 15" sign on a fence post, counts the painted pillar numbers as they go, and reaches the first tenant fit-out: a USPS loading dock with a backed-in trailer and a small commemorative-stamp display. A few hundred blocks further on, the visitor reaches the NARA archive — a dim amber-lit room with floor-to-ceiling archive shelving — and the STC data center — a sealed chamber with cool blue underglow and blinking red/green status lights. The visitor walks back to the entrance and exits through the same portal. **That is the MVP.** It is roughly 30% of the full build by block count, but it captures the soul.

## 2. Phased Delivery

The build proceeds in **six versions**, each adding a layer of completeness. Each version is a discrete deliverable that can be reviewed, played, and revised.

### v0.1 — "The Entrance"

**What it includes**:
- The ravine wall and the surface plateau (Phase 1, lite).
- The entrance ramp (Phase 6, lite).
- The first 50×50 block chamber (a quarter of the footprint, the entrance-side corner).
- The first iconic intersection (a 3×3 grid of 8×8 white concrete pillars).
- The first 50 blocks of the main avenue, with painted pillar numbers, 15 mph speed limit, stop signs, and 3 street signs (Hushpuckney + 2 invented companions).
- The portal mouth signage: "World's Largest Underground Business Complex®" sign, "SPEED LIMIT 15" sign, "NO AM/FM RECEPTION" sign.
- The Hunt Midwest visitor center, lite (a small building with a mounted sign, not a full interior).
- A small parking lot stub (3 rows × 4 cars).

**What it does NOT include**:
- The full 200×200 chamber.
- Any tenant fit-outs.
- The exit portal.
- The ghost mine.
- The inter-site connections.
- The Hunt Hall.
- Any easter eggs beyond the entrance signage.

**Play experience**:
The visitor parks in a small lot, walks down a gentle ramp into a ravine wall, and emerges into a vast painted-pillar grid. The first intersection is visible from the bottom of the ramp. The visitor walks 50 blocks down a fluorescent-lit corridor, sees the painted pillar numbers and the speed-limit sign, then turns around and walks back. **The "wow" moment is delivered, but the place is small.** Roughly 2,000–3,000 blocks; 4–6 hours of build time.

### v0.5 — "The Grid"

**What it includes**:
- The full 200×200 chamber (Phase 1, complete).
- The full pillar grid on the iconic-intersection layout (Phase 2, complete).
- The 350-block main avenue, full (Phase 3, complete).
- The 2 side spurs (100 blocks each), empty, with stub end walls.
- All 4 cross-avenues, empty, with stub end walls.
- The central plaza (intersection of Hushpuckney and Bethany Falls).
- The "SubTropolis — Est. 1964" plaque at the central plaza.
- All street signs, stop signs, and 15-mph speed limit signs at every iconic intersection.
- The Hunt Midwest visitor center, full (Phase 6, lite).
- The parking lot, full.
- The Worlds of Fun silhouette in the distance (a single dark oak fence post + white banner).

**What it does NOT include**:
- Any tenant fit-outs.
- The exit portal.
- The ghost mine.
- The inter-site connections.
- The Hunt Hall.
- The climate-controlled zones.
- The employee cafeteria, shift-change signage, etc.

**Play experience**:
The visitor drives the full main avenue from the portal to the central plaza to the (blocked) end. The grid recedes into the distance in every direction. The visitor can see the side spurs branching off the main avenue. The pillar numbers mark the way. The cross-avenues are dark and empty but the pillar grid is there. **The place feels like SubTropolis — vast, repetitive, fluorescent, painted, working — but it is empty.** Roughly 100,000–130,000 blocks; 8–12 hours of build time (in addition to v0.1).

**What it includes**:
- All 3 marquee tenant fit-outs (USPS, NARA, STC) from the design-plan §6.2.
- The secondary tenant fit-outs (Grainger, EPA, Hallmark/Russell Stover, UV&S, Ford/Grainger historical).
- The side-spur #2 historical tenants corridor (Pillsbury, Russell Stover historical, etc.).

- The interpretive wall in the visitor center (Bethany Falls limestone sample, 16-ft ceiling scale model, ENERGY STAR 100 certificate).

**What it does NOT include**:
- The exit portal.
- The ghost mine.
- The inter-site connections.
- The employee cafeteria, shift-change signage, Groundhog Run banner, 2001 USPS anthrax plaque.

**Play experience**:
The visitor drives the full main avenue, stops at each tenant fit-out, and gets out to look. The USPS dock has a backed-in trailer and a commemorative-stamp display. The NARA archive is dim and amber, with floor-to-ceiling archive shelving. The STC data center is sealed and cool-blue, with blinking red/green status lights. The Hallmark / Russell Stover combined fit-out has pallet racks and consumer goods. The UV&S vault is dim and quiet, with two labeled film cans on the middle shelf. The Hunt Hall round room has a Lamar Hunt plaque and a mounted Chiefs arrowhead. **The place is now a working city, not an empty grid.** Roughly 50,000–80,000 additional blocks; 15–20 hours of build time (in addition to v0.5).

### v2.0 — "The Complex"

The SubTropolis end of the combined-complex is complete. **The build is the canonical SubTropolis in Minecraft.** No additional block count; 4–6 hours of polish time (in addition to v1.5).

## 3. Future Extensions

The v2.0 build is the canonical SubTropolis. The following are future extensions that could be added after v2.0, in any order, as separate projects.

### 3.1 More tenant zones

The 200×200 footprint has 4 cross-avenues and 2 side spurs. v1.0 builds out 8 of the 10 most-documented tenants. Future extensions could add:

- **A wine vault / cellar** (the climate-controlled wine storage use case is widely cited; a small vault with wooden crates and a "55°F / 65% RH" sign is a nice addition).
- **A light-manufacturing fit-out** (e.g., a Pillsbury flour storage / packaging area, a Russell Stover candy wrapping area, a Hallmark ribbon-cutting area).
- **A vehicle customization tenant** (a long chamber with a few minecarts-on-rails representing a vehicle customization operation; the "truck customization businesses" are a documented SubTropolis use case).
- **A generic "Vacant / Mining Crew" chamber** (a 30×30 block chamber with a "**VACANT — MINING CREW WILL REACH THIS CHAMBER IN 2027**" sign, a single minecart, and a few ore blocks representing the future build-out).
- **A "SubTropolis Mining Museum"** (a small interpretive space off the main avenue with a 1940s mining photo (a **painting** or **item frame** with **paper**), a "**1945–1964: Limestone Mining for KC Roads and Airport Tarmacs**" sign, a single **deepslate** block labeled "**Bethany Falls Limestone Sample**").

### 3.2 More rail spurs

The deliberation cut the rail spur to a minimum (a few parked cars on a short track, or omitted). Future extensions could add:

- A short rail spur in the Ford / Grainger vehicle storage chamber, with 3–4 **minecarts with chests** representing parked rail cars.
- A small **rail yard** at the back of the grid, with a few **rails** running parallel and 2–3 **minecarts** parked on them, and a "**HUNT MIDWEST RAIL SPUR — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY**" sign.
- A "**SubTropolis Mining Co. — Rail Spur**" sign at the rail yard entrance, with a single **lantern** for atmosphere.

The rail spurs are **a real loss** from the real SubTropolis (2.1 mi of rail is documented); future extensions could partially restore the rail feel without rebuilding the full 2.1 mi.

### 3.3 Working climate control (redstone integration)

The current build has *visual* climate control (shroomlight underglow in the data center, end-rod lighting in the archive). Future extensions could add *functional* climate control:

- A **redstone lamp** network that turns the sea lanterns on the main avenue on at "night" (a redstone day/night detector) and off at "day" (the visitor sees a dimmer main avenue at night, brighter during the day).
- A **redstone lamp** network in the data center that simulates a more complex status-light flicker pattern (e.g., random redstone repeater delays on each rack).
- A **redstone clock** driving a single **note block** in the data center and another at the entrance ramp, providing a constant low hum. *Optional, advanced.* **[X]**
- A **tripwire + redstone lamp** network in the ghost mine chamber: when a player walks past a tripwire, a single **lantern** flickers on for 3 seconds, then off. The "active mining" feel.

### 3.4 A "tour mode" with NPCs / signs

The current build has in-game signs at every key location. Future extensions could add a more curated tour:

- A series of **lecterns** with **writable books** at each major location, each book containing a 1-page description of the location (the 1940s mining, the 1964 founding, the 2012 NARA lease, the 2014 STC opening, the 2026 Grainger expansion).
- A **villager** at the entrance with a **`/trigger` command** that gives the visitor a "tour guide" book when interacted with.
- A series of **note blocks** at each major location, each playing a different low note; a player walking the full tour hears a scale.
- A "**SubTropolis Tour Stops**" sign at the visitor center listing the 10 must-see locations (USPS, NARA, STC, EPA, Hallmark, UV&S, Ford/Grainger, Hunt Hall, ghost mine, exit portal).

### 3.5 Connection to the combined complex

The service tunnel and public shaft are SubTropolis-side complete in v1.5. The combined-complex project is the work of the **combined-complex report** (a separate document). Future extensions could add:

- A **rail line** through the service tunnel (the player takes a minecart from the SubTropolis service sub-basement to the Cheyenne Mountain maintenance sub-basement, and from there to the city).
- A **water elevator** in the public shaft (the player rides a boat up a column of soul-sand bubbles from the SubTropolis Public Access Lobby to the surface lobby on the valley floor, and from there walks to the city).
- A "**Combined Complex — Public Transit**" sign in the surface lobby, with a **map** item showing the three sites and the inter-site connections.
- A "**SubTropolis — Cheyenne Mountain — Houston Tunnel System**" three-site visitor center at the surface lobby, with three interpretive walls (one for each site).

## 4. Open Items for the User

The deliberation flagged six open questions. The architectural designer has proposed resolutions for all six in the design plan §15. The following are the *additional* open items the architectural designer is flagging for the human project lead.

### 4.1 Coordinate and placement decisions (depends on the site planner)

- **Ravine wall geometry**: the site planner must provide the ravine-wall orientation, the SubTropolis Y-level, the portal coordinates, and the exit portal coordinates. The architectural design assumes a south ravine wall for the main entrance and a perpendicular ravine face for the exit (per the deliberation Topic 1). If the site planner's geometry differs, the design must be adjusted.
- **Bedroom Y-level**: the site planner must confirm that the SubTropolis floor Y-level is at Y=10 or higher to avoid bedrock intersection.
- **Combined-complex integration**: the site planner must coordinate the SubTropolis service tunnel terminus (a sub-basement at Y-10) and the public shaft terminus (a Public Access Lobby on the main grid edge) with the Cheyenne Mountain and Houston tunnel-system teams.

### 4.2 Block palette decisions (depends on the Minecraft version)

- **Minecraft version**: the design uses blocks from 1.17+ (calcite, tuff, deepslate, shroomlight). If the target server is on an older version, the palette must be substituted (calcite → quartz, tuff → stone, deepslate → stone or coal, shroomlight → glowstone).
- **Resource pack**: the design assumes a vanilla or lightly-modded resource pack. If a custom resource pack is used, the **sea lantern** ceiling strips can be replaced with **glowstone** strips, the **end rod** under-shelf lighting can be replaced with **lantern** + **redstone lamp**, and the **shroomlight** under-floor can be replaced with **glowstone** under **polished deepslate** slabs. The *visual* should match; the *blocks* can be different.

### 4.3 Lighting decisions (depends on the play environment)

- **Survival vs. creative**: the design assumes creative mode (or a server with `/gamemode creative` for builders). In survival, the chamber is a mob spawn zone until lit; the build should place **sea lanterns** along the main avenue as part of Phase 1, not as a Phase 3 polish step. The "no mob spawning in the deep" rule (a fun optional rule) is a separate decision.
- **Day/night cycle**: the design's main avenue is *always* fluorescent (sea lanterns every 4–5 blocks). In survival, the player can see the difference between "inside" (constant fluorescent) and "outside" (day/night cycle) only at the portal mouth. The design assumes this is a feature, not a bug.

### 4.4 Build-tooling decisions (depends on the available tooling)

- **mc-fleet-bot capability**: the design assumes mc-fleet-bot can do `//replace`, `//walls`, `//paste`, and `setblock` operations at scale. If mc-fleet-bot has different capabilities, the working plan must be adjusted.
- **WorldEdit availability**: the design assumes WorldEdit is available on the target server. If not, the chamber carving and pillar placement can be done with `mc-fleet-bot` `setblock` operations or with vanilla `/fill` commands (slower but functional).
- **Schematic file format**: the design assumes the standard `.schem` (Sponge Schematic) format for pillar schematics. If the target server uses a different format (e.g., `.litematic` for Litematica), the pillar placement must be converted.

### 4.5 Content decisions (depends on the user's preferences)

- **The Worlds of Fun silhouette**: the design suggests a single dark oak fence post with a white banner as a faded reference. The user may prefer a more detailed abstraction (a few additional fence posts representing coaster supports, a Ferris wheel silhouette) or a *less* detailed abstraction (no silhouette at all, just a comment in the design plan).
- **The Hunt Hall arrowhead**: the design suggests a single mounted **red concrete** + **gold concrete** arrowhead (a geometric abstraction of the Chiefs logo). The user may prefer a more abstract rendering (a single gold block with a red border) or a more literal rendering (a Chiefs helmet, a football, a stadium reference — though the deliberation explicitly excludes helmet and stadium imagery).
- **The UV&S film can labels**: the design suggests "**GONE WITH THE WIND — INTERPOSITIVE**" and "**WIZARD OF OZ — MASTER**" as the labeled cans. The user may prefer different film titles, or may want the labels to be *less* specific (e.g., "**MGM — 1939**" and "**SELZNICK — 1939**" without naming the films).
- **The inter-site connection signage**: the design suggests "**SERVICE TUNNEL — COMBINED COMPLEX MAINTENANCE — AUTHORIZED VEHICLES ONLY**" and "**PUBLIC TRANSIT — COMBINED COMPLEX — TICKETED VISITORS ONLY**" as the signs at the termini. The user may want different wording, or may want the fictions to be *less* marked (the design is conservative; the user may want the fictions to integrate more smoothly with the canonical composition).

### 4.6 Timing and resource decisions (depends on the user's priorities)

- **Build priority**: the design recommends v0.1 → v0.5 → v1.0 → v1.5 → v2.0 as the natural order. The user may want to prioritize differently (e.g., v1.0 first — full tenants, no main avenue; or v1.5 first — inter-site connections for the combined-complex report).
- **Render distance flag**: the 200×200 chamber is at the edge of Minecraft render distance. The user may need to adjust the server's `view-distance` and `simulation-distance` settings to make the build navigable. The working plan flags this as a risk; the user must confirm the server settings.
- **Bot vs. human time**: the working plan estimates 32–49 hours of build time. If the user has a small team (1–2 builders) and a short timeline (1 week), the user may want to cut to the MVP (v0.1 + 3 tenant fit-outs) and defer the rest. If the user has a large team and a long timeline, the user can build to v2.0 in 2–3 weeks.

## 6. Dependencies and Hand-Off

The architectural designer's hand-offs to the next phase:

- **To the AI Contractor Writer** (downstream): the design plan (`design-plan.md`), the working plan (`working-plan.md`), and the development plan (`this document`) are the three inputs. The Contractor Writer reads the design plan for *what to build*, the working plan for *how to build it*, and the development plan for *what to build first* (the MVP). The Contractor Writer does *not* need to read the research report, the visual-assets catalog, the culture-architecture analysis, or the discussion notes; the architectural designer has already extracted the relevant binding decisions and made them explicit in the three plan documents.
- **To the human project lead** (upstream): the open items in §4 are the questions the architectural designer could not resolve without user input. The human should review these and either confirm the designer's proposed resolution or specify an alternative.
- **To the Site Planner** (parallel): the design plan assumes a south ravine wall for the main entrance, a perpendicular ravine face for the exit, a SubTropolis floor Y-level of Y=10 or higher, a service tunnel sub-basement at Y-10, and a public shaft landing at a Public Access Lobby on the grid edge. The Site Planner should confirm or adjust these assumptions in the site plan and coordinates JSON.
- **To the combined-complex team** (downstream, separate report): the SubTropolis service tunnel cross-section is ~6×5 blocks, the public shaft cross-section is ~5×5 blocks, and both termini are tagged `[X]` as project fictions. The combined-complex team should match these cross-sections at the SubTropolis end and design the Cheyenne Mountain and Houston tunnel-system ends to match.
