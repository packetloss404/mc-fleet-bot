Section 03 · What This Build Is

Two Worlds, One Integrated Minecraft Life

Two Minecraft worlds, one integrated experience. The new combined complex — now one continuous mountain, no ravine, with a horizontal granite–limestone contact at Y=200 — connects to the existing bot world (a small vanilla-384 starter base) through a Gateway portal and a 960-block coastal-plain rail spur. This is the meta-build that ties the four site-specific master plans to the workspace that has been running them for months.

The 7 binding decisions (v2.0)

  • Scale: 1 block = 1 meter globally. No compression. New world: 1,500×1,500 footprint, 1,024+ build height (CubicWorld).
  • Geometry: ONE CONTINUOUS mountain (no V-notch ravine), peak at Y=800. Horizontal granite–limestone contact at Y=200.
  • Public shaft: 7×7 cross-section, 100-block descent, mechanical lift, mid-landing at Y=−50.
  • Service tunnel: 6×6 cross-section, ~120-block length, minecart, 25-ton blast door at the Cheyenne end. Exposes the horizontal contact at Y=200 inside the tunnel.
  • Visitor journey: 60–90 minutes focused play (v2.0 lands at ~95 min — optimization open question).
  • Centerpieces: 6 + 1 (v2.0 adds the funicular summit observation platform at Y=800).
  • Easter eggs: 9. v2.0 relocates the composite terrane plaque to the service tunnel contact crossing; no ravine-specific eggs.
v2.0 design update The combined complex has dropped the V-shaped ravine. The mountain is now one continuous landform with a horizontal contact at Y=200. The return route is funicular + summit road, no skybridge. The 6 named markers use mkr_service_tunnel_contact_crossing at (−40, 200, −360) — not mkr_ravine_bottom. The composite terrane plaque lives at that contact crossing, visible from the minecart.

Section 04 · The Throughline

Two Worlds, One Experience

The map integration is the meta-build that connects the four individual site master plans to the existing workspace. It treats the new combined complex not as a standalone world but as the *next chapter* of one the bot fleet has been writing for months. The two worlds are not adjacent; they are stitched by a single architectural object — the Gateway station — and by a 960-block rail spur that carries the bot fleet's continued work across the boundary.

The two worlds, side by side

The existing bot world

  • vanilla-384 build height
  • ~200×200 explored area
  • 3 chunks, 4 active bots, 1–2 Cute houses
  • 111-schematic library on disk
  • Origin: (935, 60, 300)
  • Era: outside time (no era discipline)
  • Role: the seed, the vestibule

The new combined complex

  • 1,024+ build height (CubicWorld 2,048)
  • 1,500×1,500 footprint
  • One continuous mountain, peak at Y=800
  • Horizontal contact at Y=200, no ravine
  • Origin: (0, 0, 0) at the new city center
  • Era: 2010s → 1970s → 1964 → 1966 gradient
  • Role: the flower, the experience

The "old town" as the historical layer

The 111 schematic files in the workspace are not decoration. They are the workspace's content library — every file the bot fleet has ever built. The new world's old town re-places 33 of them at their native scales. A 245-byte cottage sits next to a 38 KB Cute house next to a 58 KB Space Mountain, and the uneven rooflines are the honest record of a library assembled from many sources over time. The visitor walks through the old town and sees — literally — the schematic library made into a place.

The Gateway station as the portkey

Two matching 7×7 pavilions — one in the existing world, one in the new world — frame a single obsidian portal. The pavilions are mirrors: one stone-brick (older, established), one wooden (newer, fresh). The portal is visual (obsidian-and-glowstone, the standard end-portal frame); the transit is a /tp command block. The Gateway is the single object that, by existing, makes the two worlds one experience.

The Grand Avenue as the urban design choice

A 425-block stone-brick-to-granite road runs from the old town plaza to the new city's SE corner. The Grand Avenue is a deliberate urban design choice in the tradition of the Champs-Élysées and the Unter den Linden — wide, straight, lined with civic ornaments, terminating at a bridge over a stream. The material transition at block 350 telegraphs the era shift from 2010s vernacular to 1970s Houston-modern.

The rail spur as the logistics layer

960 blocks of passenger minecart rail runs north-south on the new world's coastal plain, bypassing the old town on the east side, with three named stations: new world Gateway, old town, city approach. The rail is the logistics layer. The freight is the bot fleet's continued work — oak logs, cobblestone, iron, the materials the bots mine. The new world does not displace the old world; the new world is supplied by the old world.

The no-ravine, no-skybridge updates

v2.0 signature changes The return route is now funicular + summit road, no skybridge. A 600-block funicular rail climbs the granite peak from the Cheyenne outer portal (Y=200) to the summit station (Y=800) on a 1:1 incline. From the summit, an 8-landing switchback road descends 800 blocks back to the city SE corner. The composite terrane plaque is at the service tunnel contact crossing at (−40, 200, −360), with a duplicate sign at the summit road landing_5.

The "old town" as a museum of the bot fleet's work

The old town is not a normalized model village. It is a palimpsest — old buildings on top of an older city, with anchor structures that say "this was the first build here." Every re-placed schematic carries a historical sign crediting the bot fleet. The Cute houses at the center of the plaza are the founding structures, placed by the historical CuteHouse1/2 bots. The Space Mountain is the tourist attraction. The underground easter egg is the only underground build in the entire 111-file library, and it is the Combined Complex's ancestor.

The portal: visual, not functional

The obsidian-and-glowstone portal frame is the standard end-portal dimensions (4 wide × 5 tall) but it is not a functional end-portal. Section 06 · The Portkey

The Gateway Station

The Gateway is the single most important piece of integration architecture in the whole project. It is the only place where the two worlds touch. Two matching 7×7 pavilions — one in the existing world, one in the new world — frame a single, shared obsidian portal. The pavilions are mirrors: one stone-brick (the old world), one wooden (the new world). The portal is visual; the transit is a /tp command block.

Two matching pavilions, one shared portal

Real-world analogues: a border-crossing station. The Gotthard Base Tunnel's northern portal at Erstfeld is a deliberate architectural object — a glass-and-concrete pavilion that frames the entrance, with a public plaza on the surface and the tunnel mouth below. The Helsinki metro's eastern extension at Vuosaari is a similar pavilion.
