## 1. The Panel

This deliberation decides how the new 1,500 × 1,500 Combined Complex — the four-masterplan package's 800-block vertical descent through SubTropolis, the Houston tunnel, and Cheyenne Mountain — physically and narratively slots into the existing `D:\projects\mc-fleet-bot\` workspace. The existing workspace is *not* a blank canvas: it holds 113 surface schematics (1 of which is underground), a tiny live bot world at (≈935, 60, 300), and 22 JSON data files describing markers, zones, squads, missions, and bot stats. The integration is a *retrofit of meaning onto existing infrastructure*, not a greenfield design. Four voices deliberate: **the Realist** (Swiss alpine-planning architect — precedents and discipline), **the Gameplay Advocate** (will players actually traverse it?), **the Urban Planner** (does the integrated world read as *one place* with a gradient of eras?), and **the Bot Operator** (does it make the existing bot fleet system better, or just hang new things on the side?). The panel's job is not to design the build but to settle the *meta-build* — the connections, the clusters, the data layer, and the visitor journey — that the design team will inherit.

## 2. Deliberation Transcript

### Topic 1: New World vs. Retrofit

**The question:** The combined complex is 1,500 × 1,500 × 800 blocks, requires 1,024+ build height, and is designed around world origin (0, 0, 0). The existing bot world is a vanilla-384 surface area at x ≈ 800–995, y ≈ 50–71, z ≈ 220–380 — only 3 explored chunks, no underground, no mountain range, no ravine. Do we **build a new world** (recommended by research) or **retrofit the existing world** (rejected by research)?

**The Realist:** *A retrofit is fantasy. Look at the precedents: Stuttgart 21 buries 57 km of rail to free 109 hectares of surface — but the surface was already a city, and the buried infrastructure was already running. SubTropolis expands into adjacent limestone that was already part of the same deposit. Toronto PATH grew because every tunnel connected two buildings that already existed. In our case, the new mountain range is 600 × 600 blocks horizontal and 800 blocks tall, the SubTropolis chamber is 200 × 200, and Cheyenne is at Y = 250–400 inside 1,800 ft of granite. None of this exists in the existing world. The existing world is a forest clearing with a starter base. We are not integrating; we are colonizing. **Build the new world.*** The new world can *reference* the old world's content (the schematic library, the data layer), but it must be a separate container.

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *I agree with the new world, but for a different reason. If we retrofit, the existing 4 bots (Lilly, Taylor, Marcus, Hazel) are living in the same world as the new 3.5M-block combined complex. The bots would stumble into the public shaft, the service tunnel, the blast door, the 25-ton blast door at the Cheyenne end. The bots are not designed to navigate the combined complex — they're survival bots in a starter base. A new world is also a *permission boundary*: the bots stay in their world doing their thing, and the new world is the player's experience. The visitor journey is **bot base → portal → new world → old town → city → descent**, with a clean break between the two.*

**The Urban Planner:** *The new world has a critical advantage for me. The existing world has no "era" — it's a generic Minecraft forest at sea level. A new world lets us impose a **gradient of eras** from the start: the old town reads as 2010s vernacular, the new city reads as 1970s Houston-modern (the Houston tunnel's 1960s–70s boom era, per Reason 1988), the SubTropolis reads as 1964 Hunt Midwest (the real SubTropolis was carved in 1964), and Cheyenne reads as 1966 NORAD/Cheyenne Mountain. The old world is *outside time*. The new world is a deliberate historical layering. **New world, with era discipline baked in.***

**The Bot Operator:** *The new world is the only realistic option given the **schematic library is flat and scale-heterogeneous**, the **data layer is a young placeholder** (6 empty markers, 2 placeholder "Mining Area" zones, 6 empty squads, 1 walk_to_coords command), and the **existing world is too small** to host even a single masterplan centerpiece. But the *new world* must be wired into the existing bot system in a way the bots can actually use it. I want the 6 empty squads to map to 6 named squads in the new world, the 2 placeholder "Mining Area" zones to map to the SubTropolis chamber, and the existing schematics to be **loadable** in the new world (not just decorative — actually ploppable, with WorldEdit). The existing bot fleet's stats and missions need to flow into the new world too. A new world is fine, but **don't strand the existing bot system in the old world.***

**Tension points:** The Realist and Bot Operator agree on the new world but disagree on whether the *existing* world becomes vestigial (Realist: yes — it's the seed, the new world is the flower) or remains *active and integrated* (Bot Operator: yes — bots need to traverse between). The Gameplay Advocate and Urban Planner agree the new world is the *experience layer* but disagree on the role of the existing world: Gameplay says the existing world is the *vestibule* (small, lived-in, ad-hoc); Urban Planner says the existing world is *outside time* (no era) and should not appear in the new world's narrative.

**Moderator's synthesis:** All four agree the new world is necessary. The disagreement is about whether the *existing* world is a *vestibule* (vestigial but functional), a *seed* (historically meaningful), or an *active co-world* (bots operate in both). The research suggests a hybrid: the existing world is preserved as a small starter base; the 4 active bots stay there; the new world is the experience; the two are connected by a Gateway portal and a coastal-plain rail spur.

**Decision:** **Build a new 1,500 × 1,500 world with 1,024+ build height, world origin (0, 0, 0), as specified in the 04-masterplan. The existing vanilla-384 bot world is preserved as-is. The two worlds are connected by a Gateway station portal in the existing world and a matching station in the new world, with a coastal-plain rail spur as the in-new-world connector. The retrofit alternative is rejected.** Rationale: the existing world's 384-block build height and 200 × 200-block explored area are too small by ~10,000× to host the combined complex; the schematic library and data layer transfer cleanly into the new world; the new world can impose a deliberate era gradient that the existing world cannot.

### Topic 2: Old Town Composition

**The question:** The research recommends re-placing 30–40 of the 113 existing schematics in the new world's coastal plain as an "old town." The proposed 7 clusters are: residential (20 small houses), castle/fortress (3 builds), temple (3 builds), statue/ornament (10 builds), theme park (1 Space Mountain), underground easter egg (1 underground-base), Cute house anchor (1–2 historical). Which schematics? What are the 7 clusters exactly? What should NOT be in the old town?

**The Realist:** *I want the old town to read as a **deliberate real-world analogue**, not a junk drawer of the schematic library. Real-world precedents: Quebec City's Old Town (a UNESCO heritage district with strict material and height rules), Stockholm's Gamla Stan (medieval street grid, no surface parking), Bern's Altstadt (sandstone arcades). The old town should have a **single material palette** (one or two primary materials — say, oak planks and cobblestone), a **street grid** (not floating structures), and a **height cap** (no structure taller than the Cute house's 2 floors, to keep the "human scale" of an old town). Drop the theme-park builds — `Disneyland Space Mountain.schem` and `Pokémon Temple Arena.schem` are 58 KB and 63 KB respectively, dwarfing the residential cluster. The Pokemon filename is mojibake-encoded ("Pokémon Temple Arena" is rendered as garbled characters in the file system) — that's not a *build*, it's a **bug**. Theme-park content belongs on the coastal plain *outside* the old town, in a separate "attractions" zone.*

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *I want the old town to be **explorable on foot** in 10–15 minutes, with **discoverable rewards** at each cluster. Twenty houses in a row is boring — give me **named districts** with signposts. ("Welcome to Castle Row." "Welcome to Temple Hill.") The 10-statue ornament cluster is *gold* for the player — every statue is a *screenshot moment*. But 10 statues in one cluster is too many; scatter them along the Grand Avenue *and* in the old town so the player is constantly running into them. Also, the `underground-base.schem` is the **only underground build in the entire workspace** — that is the **single most interesting easter egg in the whole project**. It needs to be findable, but not obvious. Bury it under a "root cellar" entrance at the back of a residential house — the player has to *dig* to find it.*

**The Urban Planner:** *The era gradient is the whole point. The old town should read as **2010s vernacular Minecraft** — small, ad-hoc, with the 2–3 large luxury houses (`Cute house.schem`, `victorian palace.schem`) as the *anchor structures*, not the *background*. Reverse the cluster sizes: the residential cluster should be 10 small houses + 2 anchor luxury houses, the castle cluster should be 3 medium-fortress builds, the temple cluster should be 3 cultural anchors, the ornament cluster should be 6 (not 10) statues along the Grand Avenue, the theme-park cluster should be 1 build (Space Mountain, not Pokémon), the underground easter egg should be the *underground-base*, and the Cute house anchor should be 1–2 `Cute house.schem` placed at the *center* of the old town as the historical marker. **The old town is a *palimpsest* — old buildings on top of an older city, with a few anchor structures that say "this was the first build here."***

**The Bot Operator:** *I have a different concern: **the schematic library is the bot fleet's *content library*, not just decoration**. The bots have missions queued to build `birch house.schem` at (904, 79, 390) and `md castle 2.schem` at (973, 1, 453). Those are *surface* missions in the *existing* world. If we re-place those schematics in the new world's old town, the missions need to either be **re-targeted to the new world** or **marked as completed** in the existing data. The old town should not be a *museum* — it should be a **buildable district**, where the bots can place additional schematics from the library as part of the construction sequence. The data layer should track *which* schematics have been placed in the old town (probably via the markers.json or a new `placed_schematics.json` file).*

**Tension points:** Realist and Urban Planner disagree on theme-park content: Realist says drop it; Urban Planner says keep 1 (Space Mountain) as a "tourist attraction." Gameplay Advocate and Bot Operator agree the old town should be explorable but disagree on whether it's a *museum* (no bot interaction) or a *buildable district* (bots add to it). All four disagree on the number of statues/ornaments (6 vs. 10) and where they go (cluster vs. scattered along Grand Avenue).

**Moderator's synthesis:** The four-way split on count, content, and purpose is real, but a compromise is achievable: the old town has **7 clusters, 30–35 schematics total**, with the **Cute houses as the center anchor** (1–2 of them, the historical CuteHouse1/2 placement). The **theme-park content is split**: Space Mountain stays in the old town as the "tourist attraction" (1 build), Pokémon Temple Arena is *deferred* to a coastal-plain "attractions zone" *outside* the old town, partly because of the mojibake filename and partly because 63 KB is too dominant. The **statues are split**: 4 statues in the old town cluster + 6 statues along the Grand Avenue. The **underground-base is the easter egg**, partially buried, with a sign. The **bot missions are re-targeted** to the new world (the birch house and md castle 2 missions move to the old town).

**Decision:** **The old town is a 600 × 400-block coastal-plain district centered at (0, 0, 500) in the new world, with 7 clusters totaling 30–35 schematics:**
1. **Residential** (10 small houses + 2 anchor luxury houses) — center (0, 0, 480). Small houses: `birch house`, `cozy-cabin`, `cube-house`, `classic-village-house`, `mushroom-cottage`, `simple-house`, `wood-house`, `rustic-farmhouse`, `stilt-house`, `mud-house`. Anchors: `Cute house.schem` × 2 (the historical CuteHouse1/2 placement), `victorian palace.schem` × 1.
2. **Castle/fortress** (3 builds) — center (200, 0, 500). `md castle 2`, `small medieval town hall`, `stone-fortress`.
3. **Temple** (3 builds) — center (−200, 0, 500). `fantasy-temple-house`, `red-japanese-temple`, `japanese-pagoda`.
4. **Statue/ornament** (4 builds in cluster + 6 along Grand Avenue) — center (0, 0, 600) cluster. Cluster: `stone-statue`, `dragon-egg`, `giant-skull`, `snowman`. Grand Avenue (Topic 4): `teddy-bear`, `macaw-statue`, `parrot-statue`, `flying-eagle`, `villager-statue`, `enderman`.
5. **Theme park** (1 build) — center (100, 0, 600). `Disneyland Space Mountain`. (Pokémon Temple Arena is deferred to a separate "attractions zone" on the coastal plain outside the old town — see Topic 4 open question.)
6. **Underground easter egg** (1 build) — (−50, 0, 550). `underground-base.schem`, partially buried, with a sign.
7. **Cute house anchor** — folded into Residential cluster. The 2 Cute houses at the center of the old town plaza are the historical marker.
**Excluded from the old town:** `Pokémon Temple Arena.schem` (deferred; mojibake filename flagged separately), `luxury-yacht.schem` and watercraft (no water in the coastal plain), `bbq-grill.schem` and beach content (wrong era), `holiday-express-train.schem` (this is the rail spur's mode, not an old-town feature), and any schematic that is *already* used in the new city's 138 × 138 downtown (per the 04-masterplan's 8–10 generic downtown towers).

### Topic 3: The Gateway Station and Portal

**The question:** The Gateway station is a small stone-and-glass structure with a portal frame that connects the existing bot world (vanilla-384) to the new world (1,024+). What does the Gateway station look like? Where exactly is the portal? What's the visitor experience on both sides?

**The Realist:** *Real-world analogues: a border-crossing station. The Gotthard Base Tunnel's northern portal at Erstfeld is a deliberate architectural object — a glass-and-concrete pavilion that frames the entrance, with a public plaza on the surface and the tunnel mouth below. The Helsinki metro's eastern extension at Vuosaari is a similar pavilion. The Gateway station should be a **simple 7 × 7 stone-brick pavilion** with a **glass roof** (light gray stained glass), a **portal frame** (4 × 5 obsidian-and-glowstone, the standard end-portal frame, repurposed as a *visual* portal), a **sign** ("GATEWAY TO THE COMBINED COMPLEX — 0,000 m"), and a **written book** on a lectern that explains the journey. The portal is *not* a functional end portal to The End — it is a **visual frame** through which the visitor sees the new world. The actual transit is a `/tp` command block, or a separate minecart world-portal, but the *architecture* is the visual portal frame.*

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *The visitor experience is the *whole point* of the Gateway. Two requirements: (1) the visitor must understand they are *leaving* the old world and *entering* a new one, and (2) the visitor must have a *buffer zone* in both worlds where they can re-orient. On the **existing world side**: the Gateway is at the bot base, near the existing starter kit, with a rail line terminating at it. The visitor walks 30 seconds from the base, sees the pavilion, reads the sign, steps through. On the **new world side**: a *mirror* 7 × 7 wooden shelter at the same coordinates, with a sign ("YOU ARE IN THE COMBINED COMPLEX — CITY HUB"), a minecart on a 1-block-gauge rail, and a 1-block-wide dirt path leading to the Grand Avenue. The visitor exits the shelter and sees the coastal plain, the old town in the distance, and the mountain range beyond. The first 30 seconds must establish *scale*: the mountain range is 800 blocks wide, the new city is 138 × 138, the old town looks like a dollhouse. Make this **the first screenshot moment of the whole project.***

**The Urban Planner:** *The Gateway is the *seam* between the existing world (no era) and the new world (deliberate era gradient). The seam should be **architecturally loud** — the visitor should *feel* the transition. The Gateway pavilion is the **only structure in the entire project that is *both* a real-world functional object and a historical marker**. It should be clad in the *same* stone brick as the Grand Avenue (to be built), the *same* obsidian as the portal, and the *same* glass as the new city's skyscrapers. It is the **palette anchor** for the whole new world. The 2 Cute houses at the old town center are the *first* buildings in the new world, but the Gateway is the *first architectural object* the visitor sees.*

**The Bot Operator:** *Two technical requirements the design team must own: (1) the portal must be **flippable** — the visitor can go *back* through the portal from the new world to the existing world, and the experience on both sides is the same. The existing world's Gateway must have the *same* pavilion, the *same* portal frame, the *same* sign, the *same* written book. (2) The data layer must record the Gateway's location in *both* worlds. Recommend: 2 new markers in `markers.json` — `mkr_gateway_existing_world` at the bot base's southern edge (e.g., (935, 60, 280)) and `mkr_gateway_new_world` at the new world's southern coastal plain (e.g., (0, 0, 700)). The 2 placeholder markers that already exist in the data layer can be reused for these.*

**Tension points:** Realist and Gameplay Advocate disagree on whether the portal is a *visual* frame or a *functional* end portal. Realist says visual (architecture-led); Gameplay says it should be a *placeable* minecart-ride experience (ride the rail spur out, arrive at the new world station on a minecart). Bot Operator and Urban Planner agree on the *seam* metaphor but disagree on the *palette*: Urban Planner says the Gateway is the palette anchor (stone brick + obsidian + glass); Bot Operator says the data layer records the Gateway's location, which is a *different* kind of seam.

**Moderator's synthesis:** The Gateway is the *single most important piece of integration architecture* in the whole project — it is the only place where the two worlds touch. The compromise: the Gateway is a **7 × 7 stone-brick pavilion with a glass roof, an obsidian portal frame, a sign, and a written book**. The portal is *visually* an end-portal frame but *functionally* a `/tp` command block or a separate minecart world-spawn anchor. The visitor experience is **symmetric**: the existing world has a Gateway pavilion at (935, 60, 280), the new world has a matching wooden shelter at (0, 0, 700), connected by a 1-block-gauge rail spur (Topic 5). The data layer records both with markers. The 2 existing placeholder markers are reused.

**Decision:**
- **Existing world Gateway:** 7 × 7 stone-brick pavilion at (935, 60, 280), 3 blocks south of the existing bot base. Glass roof (light gray stained glass), obsidian portal frame (4 × 5), sign reading "GATEWAY TO THE COMBINED COMPLEX — 0,000 m", written book on a lectern with the visitor journey. The portal frame is a *visual* marker; the actual transit is a `/tp` command block hidden inside the frame.
- **New world Gateway (mirror):** 7 × 7 wooden shelter at (0, 0, 700), new world's southern coastal plain. Same obsidian portal frame, sign reading "YOU ARE IN THE COMBINED COMPLEX — CITY HUB — 0,000 m FROM CITY CENTER", a minecart on a 1-block-gauge rail, and a 1-block-wide dirt path leading to the Grand Avenue.
- **The portal is *visual*, not a functional end-portal.** The actual transit is a `/tp` command (or a separate "world-portal" datapack if the bot team has one). The pavilion is the *architecture*; the transit is the *mechanism*.
- **Data layer:** 2 new markers in `markers.json` — `mkr_gateway_existing_world` at (935, 60, 280), `mkr_gateway_new_world` at (0, 0, 700). The 2 existing placeholder markers in the data layer are *reused* for these (instead of adding new ones).
- **Bot team must implement the world-transit mechanism.** The `/tp` command or world-portal datapack is the design team's responsibility. The architecture is specified; the mechanism is the bot team's.

### Topic 4: The Grand Avenue

**The question:** The Grand Avenue is a 425-block stone-brick road linking the old town to the new city. It should be wide, lined with statues/ornaments, and feel like a deliberate urban design choice. How wide is the Grand Avenue? What statues/ornaments line it? Is it a straight line or does it curve?

**The Realist:** *A "Grand Avenue" in the real world is a *deliberate urban design choice*, not just a wide road. Look at the Champs-Élysées (Paris, 71 m wide, 1.9 km long, lined with plane trees, terminating at the Arc de Triomphe), the Unter den Linden (Berlin, 60 m wide, 1.5 km long, lined with linden trees and palaces), the Avenue de la Paz (Mexico City), the Mall (Washington DC, 70 m wide but a *green* mall, terminating at the Lincoln Memorial). The Grand Avenue should be **straight** (real grand avenues are straight — the curvature is reserved for *gardens* and *parkways*), **wide** (8–10 blocks total cross-section: 4-block road + 2-block sidewalk each side + 1-block planter each side), and **lined with the 6 statuary ornaments** (Topic 2 Decision: `teddy-bear`, `macaw-statue`, `parrot-statue`, `flying-eagle`, `villager-statue`, `enderman`) at 70-block intervals. Cross a small stream at the SE corner of the city with a stone-brick bridge.*

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *Straight is fine, but **the visitor should never be bored**. 425 blocks of straight road is a 4–5 minute minecart ride. Add **3 signposted milestones** along the way: (1) at block 100, "OLD TOWN 1/4 MILE — Castle Row"; (2) at block 250, "OLD TOWN CENTER — Cute House Plaza"; (3) at block 350, "CITY APPROACHING — Stream Crossing 100 m". And **5 of the 6 statues** should be *interactive* in some way — a chest inside the teddy bear, a sign at the macaw, a "Press button for tour" lever at the villager-statue. The enderman is the **boss of the Grand Avenue** — a 1.6-block-tall statue at the city approach, with a sign reading "ENTER THE CITY". The Grand Avenue is the **player's onboarding corridor** to the new city; treat it like a real-world *promenade*.*

**The Urban Planner:** *The Grand Avenue is the *seam between the old town and the new city*, and it must **visually announce the transition**. The old town has a 2-floor height cap; the new city has 4 anchor towers at 80 blocks. The Avenue should *gradually* scale up: at the old town end, the surrounding structures are 2 floors; at the city end, the skyscrapers and the Wells Fargo-style descent are visible. The road should be **stone brick for the first 350 blocks**, then **transition to the new city's granite-and-glass material** for the final 75 blocks. The cross-section is the same (8–10 blocks), but the *materials* telegraph the era shift. The 6 statuary ornaments should be **renamed as "founding-era markers"** with signs explaining they were placed in the era of the bot fleet's first schematic library. The Grand Avenue is the **historical corridor** that proves the old town and the new city are *one place*.*

**The Bot Operator:** *425 blocks is a **real rail distance** — the existing world's explored area is only 200 × 200 blocks. The Grand Avenue is *larger than the entire existing bot world*. The bots cannot patrol it. Recommend: the Grand Avenue is **not bot-patrolled** (it is a *visual* feature); the **statues are not bot-interactive** (they are decorative); the data layer records the Grand Avenue as a **single marker** (`mkr_grand_avenue_center`) and a **named route** (`rte_grand_avenue`) in `routes.json` (a new file). The 5 "interactive" statues from the Gameplay Advocate are **not interactive** in the bot sense — they are *static* decorative builds. The bot fleet's role in the Grand Avenue is *zero*; it is a player-only feature.*

**Tension points:** Realist and Gameplay disagree on whether the Grand Avenue is a *real-world urban design* (Realist: trees, planters, classical proportions) or a *playable corridor* (Gameplay: milestones, interactive statues, signs). Urban Planner and Bot Operator disagree on whether the Avenue is a *historical* feature (with founding-era signs) or a *decorative* feature (with no bot interaction). All four agree on **straight, wide, 8–10-block cross-section, with statues at intervals**.

**Moderator's synthesis:** The Grand Avenue is **straight, 8-block total cross-section (4-block road + 2-block sidewalk each side, no planters — the research recommends 1-block planters but the panel cuts them to keep the cross-section Minecraft-pedestrian-friendly at 8 blocks), stone brick for the first 350 blocks, granite-and-glass for the final 75 blocks**. The 6 statuary ornaments (`teddy-bear`, `macaw-statue`, `parrot-statue`, `flying-eagle`, `villager-statue`, `enderman`) are placed at 70-block intervals along the Avenue, with **founding-era signs** explaining they are from the bot fleet's first schematic library. The 3 signposted milestones (old town, plaza, city approach) are the Gameplay Advocate's compromise. The Avenue is **decorative, not bot-interactive**, and the data layer records a single marker and a single named route. The **enderman is the "boss"** at the city approach, with a sign reading "ENTER THE CITY".

**Decision:**
- **Cross-section:** 8 blocks total — 4-block stone-brick road (2 blocks each direction) + 2-block oak-plank sidewalk each side. (No planters; cross-section is intentionally Minecraft-pedestrian-friendly, not Champs-Élysées-grand.)
- **Length:** 425 blocks, from old town center (0, 0, 500) to SE corner of the new city (60, 0, 70). Straight line.
- **Material transition:** stone brick for blocks 0–350 (old-town era), granite-and-glass for blocks 350–425 (city-era, mirroring the city's skyscraper material).
- **Statues:** 6 statues at 70-block intervals — `teddy-bear` (block 70), `macaw-statue` (block 140), `parrot-statue` (block 210), `flying-eagle` (block 280), `villager-statue` (block 350, on the material transition), `enderman` (block 420, "boss" at city approach). Each statue has a founding-era sign.
- **Milestones:** 3 signposts at blocks 100, 250, 350 — "OLD TOWN 1/4 MILE", "OLD TOWN CENTER — CUTE HOUSE PLAZA", "CITY APPROACHING — STREAM CROSSING 100 m".
- **Stream crossing:** stone-brick bridge at block 380, 5-block-wide arch over a 3-block-wide stream.
- **Data layer:** 1 new marker `mkr_grand_avenue_center` at (0, 0, 285); 1 new named route `rte_grand_avenue` in a new `routes.json` file (which doesn't exist yet in the data layer — to be created by the design team).

### Topic 5: The Coastal-Plain Rail Spur

**The question:** The coastal-plain rail spur is a 3-block cross-section rail line from the new city S edge (Z = +70) → through the new world's old town → via portal to the existing bot base. 960 blocks total, ~20K blocks, 1-block-gauge rail, powered rail every 8 blocks. Confirm the cross-section, the gauge, the route, the mode. Is this a freight or passenger rail? Does it stop at the Gateway station?

**The Realist:** *A rail spur is *not* a metro line; it is a *connective* piece of infrastructure. The research recommends a 1-block-gauge rail, but real-world precedents suggest we should be more deliberate: the Gotthard Base Tunnel uses standard gauge (1,435 mm = ~3 blocks at 1:1); the Toronto PATH uses *no* rail (it's pedestrian-only); the Houston tunnel uses *no* rail (pedestrian-only). The 1-block-gauge is a *Minecraft convention*, not a real-world one. I will accept the 1-block-gauge for playability, but I want the **cross-section to be deliberate**: 1-block rail (powered rail on the center line) + 1-block walkway on the east side + 1-block grass/utility strip on the west side. **Passenger, not freight** — the rail spur carries visitors, not cargo. **Stops at the Gateway station** (both ends) and at the **old town station** (mid-route).*

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *The rail spur is **the most important piece of gameplay infrastructure in the new world**. 960 blocks is a 5–7 minute minecart ride — that's the player's *first time* seeing the new world. It must be **fun, scenic, and continuous** (no derailing). Powered rail every 8 blocks keeps the cart at full speed; the player just *sits and watches*. The spur should **pass through 3 named stations**: (1) the **new world Gateway station** at (0, 0, 700), (2) the **old town station** at (0, 0, 500), and (3) the **city approach station** at (0, 0, 70). The spur does NOT enter the city proper — the visitor disembarks at the city approach and walks the Grand Avenue. The spur is **passenger, not freight**, and it is the player's *onboarding corridor* to the new world.*

**The Urban Planner:** *The rail spur is a **diagonal that ties the linear Grand Avenue to the orthogonal city grid**. The new city's site plan is rectangular, with a 138 × 138 block downtown. The old town is rectangular, with a 600 × 400 coastal-plain block. The rail spur is the *connector* that runs north-south between them, then bends east-west to the existing world. ### Topic 7: Visitor Journey

**The question:** The visitor has two origin points: the new world (most of the build) and the existing bot world (the historical layer). The full journey is **bot base → portal → Gateway station → old town → Grand Avenue → new city → public shaft → SubTropolis → service tunnel → Cheyenne**, potentially 60–90 minutes of focused play. Which origin point is the "default" spawn? How do we make the full journey coherent? Are there any shortcuts (like fast travel) for the return?

**The Realist:** *The visitor journey is a **pilgrimage**, not a commute. Real-world analogues: the Aljafería palace in Zaragoza (a single building with 9 layers of history, from the 11th-century Islamic palace to the 19th-century barracks); the Vatican Museums (a 6-km linear path through 2,000 years of art); the Sasso da Pigna / La Claustra (a 2,400-m gallery tour through a former Swiss bunker). The visitor should enter at the **existing world bot base** — this is the *vestibule*, the small ad-hoc world, and the visitor must feel the *contrast* between the small existing world and the vast new world. The journey is **one-way** for the descent (60–90 minutes of focused play is the *intended* experience), with **a single return shortcut** at the end — a portal at the Cheyenne outer portal that takes the visitor back to the Gateway station in the existing world. The return shortcut is a *deliberate architectural object*, not a `/tp` command — it is a portal frame in the Cheyenne outer portal that reads "RETURN TO GATEWAY."*

**The Gameplay Advocate:** *The journey is too long. 60–90 minutes is **fine for a one-time exploration**, but the visitor will want to *return* to specific locations. Three requirements: (1) the **default spawn is the new world** (the visitor arrives at the new world Gateway station, not the existing bot base — this is the player's *first* experience, and the existing world is a *secondary* visit). (2) **Fast travel is required**: the visitor can `/tp` (or use a command block) to any of the 6 named markers (`mkr_city_center`, `mkr_public_shaft_top`, `mkr_subtropolis_chamber_center`, `mkr_service_tunnel_contact_crossing`, `mkr_cheyenne_outer_portal`, `mkr_old_town_center`). Fast travel is *part of the experience* — the visitor can re-visit the old town, the city, the SubTropolis, the service tunnel contact crossing, the Cheyenne chamber without re-walking the full journey. (3) The **return shortcut at the end** is a portal at the Cheyenne outer portal that takes the visitor back to the Gateway station — Realist's compromise, agreed.*

**The Urban Planner:** *The journey is a **gradient of eras**, and the era discipline must be visible at every stage. The existing world is *outside time*. The Gateway station is the *seam* — the visitor crosses it and is *in* the era gradient. The old town is 2010s vernacular. The Grand Avenue transitions 2010s → 1970s. The new city is 1970s Houston-modern. The SubTropolis is 1964 Hunt Midwest. The Cheyenne chamber is 1966 NORAD. The visitor must **see the era shift at every transition** — material palette changes, sign typography changes, lighting changes. The default spawn is **the new world Gateway station** (Gameplay's compromise), but the visitor can also choose to spawn in the **existing bot base** via a server-side option. The full journey is **one-way for the descent** (Realist), but the **return is a portal at the Cheyenne outer portal** (Realist + Gameplay), and **fast travel is available to all 6 named markers** (Gameplay).*

**The Bot Operator:** *The journey is **a 60–90 minute linear path through 6 named markers**, and the data layer must record every step. Requirements: (1) the **default spawn is the new world** (Gameplay compromise, agreed). (2) **All 6 named markers are fast-travel destinations** via a `/tp` command or a command-block menu. (3) The **return portal at the Cheyenne outer portal** is a *visual* portal frame, with a `/tp` command block inside that takes the visitor to the existing-world Gateway station. (4) The data layer records **the full journey as a single named route** in `routes.json`: `rte_visitor_journey_full` from `mkr_new_world_gateway` → `mkr_old_town_center` → `mkr_grand_avenue_center` → `mkr_city_center` → `mkr_public_shaft_top` → `mkr_subtropolis_chamber_center` → `mkr_service_tunnel_contact_crossing` → `mkr_cheyenne_outer_portal`. (5) **The existing world's bot base is *not* on the visitor journey** — it is the *origin* (where the visitor can `/tp` *from* the existing world to the new world), but the visitor does not return to the existing world mid-journey. The return is a single portal at the Cheyenne outer portal.*

**Tension points:** Realist and Gameplay disagree on the **default spawn**: Realist says the existing world (the visitor must *feel* the contrast); Gameplay says the new world (the visitor's first experience should be the new world, not the small existing one). Realist and Bot Operator disagree on whether the **return is a single portal** (Realist: yes) or **fast travel to all 6 markers** (Bot Operator: yes, but only after the first descent). All four agree on the **return portal at the Cheyenne outer portal**.

**Moderator's synthesis:** The visitor journey is a **60–90 minute linear descent from the new world Gateway station to the Cheyenne outer portal**, with **a single return portal at the end**. The **default spawn is the new world Gateway station** (Gameplay compromise, Realist agrees because the existing world is a *secondary* visit). The **6 named markers are fast-travel destinations** (Gameplay + Bot Operator), but **fast travel is only available *after* the first descent** (Realist: the first time, the visitor must walk the journey). The **return portal at the Cheyenne outer portal** is a visual portal frame with a `/tp` command block, taking the visitor to the existing-world Gateway station. The data layer records the full journey as a single named route.

**Decision:**
- **Default spawn:** the new world Gateway station at (0, 0, 700). The existing-world bot base is a *secondary* visit (the visitor can `/tp` from the existing world to the new world via the Gateway, but the new world is the *primary* experience).
- **Full journey (one-way descent, 60–90 minutes):** `mkr_new_world_gateway` (0, 0, 700) → rail spur to `mkr_spur_old_town` (0, 0, 500) → walk through old town plaza → Grand Avenue (425 blocks) to `mkr_grand_avenue_center` (0, 0, 285) → continue to `mkr_city_center` (0, 0, 0) → city streets to `mkr_public_shaft_top` (60, 0, −70) → public shaft descent (100 blocks) to `mkr_subtropolis_chamber_center` (0, −50, −200) → service tunnel (80–120 blocks) through `mkr_service_tunnel_contact_crossing` (0, −250, −360) to `mkr_cheyenne_outer_portal` (0, 0, −420).
- **Return shortcut:** a single portal frame at the Cheyenne outer portal, with a `/tp` command block inside, taking the visitor to the existing-world Gateway station at (935, 60, 280). The portal is *visual* (obsidian-and-glowstone frame) but the transit is a `/tp`.
- **Fast travel:** after the first descent, the visitor can `/tp` to any of the 6 named markers (`mkr_city_center`, `mkr_public_shaft_top`, `mkr_subtropolis_chamber_center`, `mkr_service_tunnel_contact_crossing`, `mkr_cheyenne_outer_portal`, `mkr_old_town_center`). Fast travel is *not available* on the first descent — the visitor must walk the journey the first time.
- **Data layer:** 1 new named route `rte_visitor_journey_full` in `routes.json` (covers the full 6-marker descent); 6 markers already specified (Topic 5 + 04-masterplan) are the fast-travel destinations; 1 new return portal marker `mkr_cheyenne_return_portal` at (0, 0, −420).

## 4. Tensions and Tradeoffs Acknowledged

- **Pokémon Temple Arena deferred, not dropped.** The filename is mojibake-encoded and the build is 63 KB — too dominant for the old town. We chose to defer it to a separate coastal-plain "attractions zone" rather than force-fit it. Tradeoff: the project now has *two* coastal-plain districts (old town + attractions zone), which complicates the era discipline. Mitigation: the attractions zone is *outside* the old town's 600 × 400 footprint and uses a different material palette (smooth stone + quartz) to mark it as a different era.

- **Existing world is the *vestibule*, not the *seed*.** The Realist argued the existing world is the seed of the new world; the Gameplay Advocate and Bot Operator argued it is the vestibule. We chose **vestibule** for the default spawn (the visitor enters the new world first) but **seed** for the data layer (the 2 placeholder "Mining Area" zones, the 6 empty markers, the 6 empty squads, the schematic library are all *re-used* in the new world, not abandoned). Tradeoff: the existing world is *less prominent* in the visitor experience than the research originally proposed. Mitigation: the existing-world Gateway pavilion is a *deliberate architectural object* (7 × 7 stone-brick with glass roof, obsidian portal frame, sign, written book) so the visitor does not experience the existing world as a *throwaway*.

- **No bot interaction with the Grand Avenue or the statues.** The Gameplay Advocate wanted 5 of the 6 statues to be *interactive* (chests, levers, signs); the Bot Operator said the Grand Avenue is *decorative, not bot-interactive*. We chose **decorative** (the statues have founding-era signs but no chests or levers) to keep the data layer simple. Tradeoff: the player has fewer *interactive rewards* on the Grand Avenue. Mitigation: the 3 milestones and the enderman "boss" at the city approach provide *discoverable moments* without requiring bot-maintained interactions.

- **Fast travel only after the first descent.** The Realist argued the visitor must walk the full 60–90 minute journey the first time; the Gameplay Advocate wanted fast travel from the start. We chose **walk first, fast travel after** to preserve the *pilgrimage* quality. Tradeoff: a player who joins after the first descent will not experience the full journey. Mitigation: the data layer records the full journey as a single named route, and the design team can publish a *"guided tour"* datapack for new players.

- **The 5 new markers + 1 new route are the *minimum* data layer.** The Bot Operator wanted markers for *all* new features (the 4 stations, the 3 milestones, the 6 statues, the 1 easter egg, etc.). We chose **the minimum** (6 new markers, 2 new routes) to keep the data layer legible. Tradeoff: the data layer is *less detailed* than the bot operator proposed. Mitigation: the design team can add more markers as the build progresses; the minimum is the *contract*, not the ceiling.

## 5. Open Questions for the User (the architect)

1. **The Pokémon Temple Arena schematic.** It is 63 KB, has a mojibake-encoded filename, and is *deferred* to a separate coastal-plain attractions zone. **Is the deferral acceptable, or should it be dropped entirely?** A 63 KB build is larger than the entire residential cluster of the old town.

2. **The schematics inspector.** The `underground-base.schem` (1,048 bytes) is the only underground build in the entire workspace, and we don't know its exact contents. The design team must inspect it before placing it. **Is there a schematic-inspector tool already in the workspace, or does the design team need to build one?** The `.schem` format is gzipped NBT; a simple Python or Node.js script can parse it.

3. **The legacy block ID risk.** The `underground-base.schem` may contain blocks that no longer exist in the current Minecraft world version. The design team must test the schematic in a sandbox before committing. **Is there a sandbox world available, or should the design team create one?** The existing bot world is a *production* world; testing in it is not safe.

4. **The world-transit mechanism.** The Gateway portal is *visual* (obsidian frame) but the *transit* is a `/tp` command block (or a separate "world-portal" datapack). **Does the bot team have a world-portal datapack, or should the design team implement the `/tp` command block manually?** A `/tp` command block is the simplest solution, but a world-portal datapack is more elegant.

5. **The new world's spawn point.** The 04-masterplan specifies world origin (0, 0, 0), but Minecraft's default spawn for a new world depends on the world type (superflat, default, amplified, etc.). **Should the new world be a superflat world (clean, predictable) or a default world (with terrain generation, biomes, etc.)?** The combined complex requires *specific* terrain (mountain range, coastal plain), so a default world may be more flexible. But a superflat world is easier to manage.

6. **The existing world's relationship to the new world after the integration.** The Bot Operator wanted the existing world to remain *active* (bots operating in both); the Realist wanted it to become *vestigial* (bots only in the new world). We chose **active, but secondary** (bots stay in the existing world; the new world is the player experience). **Is this the right call, or should the bots be migrated to the new world?** Migrating the bots is a *significant* data-layer operation (the bots' stats, missions, blackboard reservations, affinities would all need to be re-targeted to the new world).
