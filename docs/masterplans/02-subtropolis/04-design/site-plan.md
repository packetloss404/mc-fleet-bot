# SubTropolis — Site Plan

> **Master site plan for the SubTropolis Minecraft replica build.**
> Macro site design: where the build sits, how the terrain arranges, what the visitor sees.
> Scale: ~200×200 block footprint, 1:1 vertical (5-block ceiling, 8×8 pillars), ~350-block main avenue.
> Position: south wall of a deep ravine/gorge, opposite Cheyenne Mountain Complex on the north wall.
> This document feeds the Architectural Designer and the AI Contractor Writer downstream.

---

## 1. Site Overview

The SubTropolis replica is carved into the **south wall of a deep ravine/gorge** that runs through the same mountain range as Cheyenne Mountain. A visitor driving up from the Kansas City plain sees a small modern surface building set into a tree-covered bluff, with a paved road descending into the hillside at shallow grade. That single, almost-unremarkable portal is the entrance to a 200×200 block underground world of painted-limestone pillars, fluorescent lighting, paved streets with geology-themed names, and 55+ working tenants — the world's largest underground business complex, compressed to a buildable scale. The mine extends into the hillside (north) from this south-face portal, and exits through a second portal on the **east face** of the same hill, perpendicular to the entrance. The ravine is to the north; the city in the broader valley is to the south. Across the ravine, ~400–600 blocks away, the Cheyenne Mountain Complex sits on the north wall, accessed by a service tunnel that runs through the bottom of the ravine and by a public shaft that climbs from the SubTropolis grid up the ravine wall to the city on the valley floor.

**Coordinates / origin.** World origin **(0, 70, 0)** is the main entrance portal at surface level on the south face of the SubTropolis hill. **North = −Z** (the mine extends into negative Z; the ravine is further negative Z). **East = +X**. **Up = +Y**. The build's Y levels are anchored to sea level (Y=64); the portal sits at Y=70 (just above sea level on the south face), the main grid floor is at Y=20 (50 blocks below the portal, 1:1 to the 150 ft depth), the surface plateau is at Y=80, the top of the hill is at Y=120, and the ravine floor is at Y=0–10.

## 2. Terrain & Geology (Minecraft-Translated)

### 2.1 The SubTropolis hill (a bluff, not a peak)

SubTropolis is a **mine carved into a hillside/bluff**, not a bunker carved into a mountain peak. The hill rises from the broader Missouri River valley (south) to a height of ~120 blocks; the ravine is carved into its north face. The mine is dug horizontally INTO this hill from the south face, not vertically down a shaft.

**Composition: Bethany Falls limestone.** Cream/beige limestone with characteristic horizontal layering (sedimentary strata, ~270 Ma). The Minecraft translation: 70% smooth stone, 15% calcite, 10% diorite, 5% tuff, with occasional deepslate veins. Strata should be visible as horizontal bands, especially in the rough-hewn walls of the unfinished chambers.

**Surrounding terrain:**
- **South (positive Z):** the Kansas City plain / Missouri River valley. Gentle rolling hills, grass, dirt, scattered trees. The "drive up" approach.
- **North (negative Z):** the deep ravine. The ravine is ~400–600 blocks across and descends to a floor at Y=0–10. The Cheyenne Mountain Complex sits on the north wall of the ravine.
- **East/West:** the hill extends in both directions along the ravine wall. The second exit portal is on the east face.
- **Above (positive Y):** the surface plateau on top of the hill, with a small visitor center, a parking lot stub, and the public-shaft surface exit.

### 2.2 The surface above the mine

The surface plateau at Y=80 is **deliberately underwhelming**: a small modern office building, a parking lot, a security gate, a paved access road. A few trees, a Lamar Hunt / KC Chiefs plaque, and a single roller-coaster or Ferris-wheel silhouette in the distance (faded reference to the Worlds of Fun amusement park that sits above the real SubTropolis). The 1,100-acre real SubTropolis surface footprint compresses to a 200×200 block plateau — the design feature is the *smallness*. **The largest underground business complex in the world is, from the surface, almost nothing.**

- **Location:** (0, 70, 0) — the world origin. The portal opens from the south face of the hill, facing the visitor center and the Kansas City plain.
- **Surface building:** a small modern office, the Hunt Midwest / SubTropolis leasing office. Branded sign: "Hunt Midwest SubTropolis — World's Largest Underground Business Complex ®". [D]

- **Clearance:** portal frame is 12 blocks wide × 5 blocks high (1:1 to 40 ft × 16 ft). [D]

### 3.2 The second exit portal

- **Location:** (100, 70, −100) — the east face of the SubTropolis hill, on the perpendicular face. [D]
- **Configuration:** same 12×5 portal frame, a smaller surface lot, an exit-only security booth. The drive-through is publicly accessible: visitors enter one portal, drive through, and exit the other. [D]

## 5. Visitor Flow & Circulation

The build is choreographed as a staged experience. Each stage specifies what the visitor sees, the light, the sound, and the block underfoot.

### Stage 1 — Drive-up (the Kansas City plain)
- **Setting:** rolling plains to the south (positive Z), grass/dirt, scattered trees, full daylight, surface ambient.
- **Visible:** a small modern building set into a tree-covered bluff, a paved access road leading to a security gate.
- **What the visitor feels:** this is a normal surface environment. The portal is unremarkable.

### Stage 2 — Surface entrance
- **Setting:** the visitor center + parking lot, a security booth, the portal mouth visible against the south face of the hill.
- **Visible:** the "SubTropolis" marquee sign, tenant directory, "SPEED LIMIT 15," clearance warning.
- **What the visitor feels:** still surface, but the scale is wrong. The portal is bigger than expected. The bluff is taller than expected.

### Stage 3 — Descent
- **Setting:** the paved two-lane road slopes down into the hillside. The sky disappears within 30–50 blocks. Fluorescent strip lighting becomes visible at the bottom of the ramp.
- **Light transition:** from full daylight to a cooler, dimmer ambient. The change is *clean and immediate* — within 10 blocks, the visitor knows they are underground.
- **What the visitor feels:** mild disorientation. The air is the same Minecraft air, but the *light* is different.

### Stage 4 — Arrival (the pillar grid reveal)
- **Setting:** the bottom of the entrance ramp, where the road levels out at the south edge of the main grid. The first major intersection is visible: a 3×3 grid of 8×8 white-painted pillars extending in every direction. Fluorescent overhead. A "Hushpuckney Ave" street sign. A "WELCOME TO SUBTROPOLIS" sign on the nearest pillar.
- **What the visitor feels:** the *wow* moment. The regularity, the scale, the painted columns, the fluorescent lights — this is the iconic image. **Disproportionate design effort should go here.**

### Stage 5 — Main avenues
- **Setting:** the visitor walks or drives down a 200-block main avenue (Hushpuckney / Bethany Falls), lined with pillar after pillar. Tenant fit-outs visible on either side. The avenue is straight; the pillars are evenly spaced; the ceiling is flat. The repetition *is* the impression.
- **What the visitor feels:** the second iconic moment, after the reveal. The scale. The "this is bigger than I expected" feeling.

### Stage 6 — Tenant zone visits
The visitor encounters 6+ distinct tenant fit-outs along the main avenue:
1. **USPS Stamp Distribution Center** — loading dock with dock doors, USPS livery, a small commemorative-stamp display.
2. **NARA Federal Records Center** — climate-controlled archive, dim amber lighting, rows of archive boxes.
3. **SubTropolis Technology Center** — sealed data center, server racks, red/green status lights, biometric hand-reader.
4. **EPA Region 7** — emergency-response signage, smaller fit-out.
5. **Hallmark / Russell Stover** — packaging-line tenant fit-out.
6. **W.W. Grainger** — long chamber with rows of vehicles or pallet racking, parking-bay markings.
7. **UV&S film archive** — small vault with film canisters, single labeled can.
8. **Hunt Hall** — round room with a Lamar Hunt interpretive plaque and a mounted Chiefs arrowhead.

### Stage 7 — The ghost mine
- **Setting:** a side corridor blocked by a barricade with "DANGER — ACTIVE MINING." Beyond: dark, raw rock, no lights, a parked mine cart.
- **What the visitor feels:** the contrast between developed and undeveloped. Most of the 55 M sq ft is unfinished.

### Stage 8 — Climate-controlled vault zone
- **Setting:** NARA at (50, 20, −150) and UV&S at (−50, 20, −180). Dim amber lighting. Climate-control signage. Archive boxes. Film cans.
- **What the visitor feels:** the "this is why tenants rent here" feeling. Constant climate = archival value.

### Stage 9 — Public Access Lobby (combined-complex exit)
- **Setting:** (60, 20, −180), a small lobby with a security guard booth, a turnstile, and a visible vertical shaft going up. Sign reads "PUBLIC TRANSIT — COMBINED COMPLEX."
- **What the visitor feels:** a transition point. The grid is over. The next stop is the city (via the public shaft) or Cheyenne (via the service tunnel at the NW sub-basement).

### Stage 10 — Exit portal
- **Setting:** the visitor turns back to the main avenue, drives east to (95, 20, −100), ascends the exit ramp, and emerges through the second portal at (100, 70, −100) onto the east face of the hill.
- **What the visitor feels:** the daylight returns. The drive-through is complete. From the east face, looking back, the SubTropolis hill is unremarkable — a small surface building, a parking lot, a tree-covered bluff.

## 6. Combined-Complex Integration

The SubTropolis replica is one of three builds in the combined-complex project. The combined-complex team coordinates the inter-site features as follows:

### 6.1 Position relative to the ravine

- **SubTropolis:** south wall of the ravine. The hill rises from the Kansas City plain (south) to ~Y=120. The ravine cuts into the north face. The main entrance is on the south face (facing the plain); the second exit is on the east face.
- **Cheyenne Mountain Complex:** north wall of the ravine, ~400–600 blocks away across the ravine floor.
- **Houston tunnel system:** in the city in the broader valley, beyond the SubTropolis hill. Accessed via the public shaft or by surface travel from the SubTropolis plateau.

### 6.2 Geology: limestone (SubTropolis) vs granite (Cheyenne)

The mountain range is geologically varied: the SubTropolis hill is **Bethany Falls limestone** (cream/beige, sedimentary, layered), and Cheyenne Mountain is **granite** (gray, igneous, crystalline). Both exploitations of the same mountain range, but very different rocks. The Minecraft translation:
- **SubTropolis hill:** smooth stone, calcite, diorite, tuff (sedimentary feel). [D]
- **Cheyenne Mountain:** granite, polished granite, andesite (igneous feel). [D cross-ref]
- The two builds should read as **geologically distinct** even though they're in the same range.

### 6.3 Service tunnel (SubTropolis ↔ Cheyenne)

- **Path:** horizontal, 6×5 block cross-section, running under the ravine floor at Y=4–9 (sub-basement level).
- **SubTropolis terminus:** (−80, 10, −180) — a service sub-basement below the main grid, accessed by a downward corridor with a security gate. Single-vehicle turnaround, maintenance bay, signage "SERVICE TUNNEL — COMBINED COMPLEX MAINTENANCE — AUTHORIZED VEHICLES ONLY."
- **Cheyenne terminus:** designed by the Cheyenne Mountain team; cross-section must match (6×5).
- **Vehicle support:** minecart-compatible for the player; visually designed for a small service vehicle.
- **Tag:** [X] in both master plans and the combined-complex report.

### 6.4 Public shaft (SubTropolis → city)

- **Path:** vertical, 5×5 block cross-section, running from the SubTropolis grid at Y=20 up through the ravine wall to a surface exit on the SubTropolis hilltop at Y=90.
- **SubTropolis terminus (Public Access Lobby):** (60, 20, −180) — a small lobby with a security guard booth, a turnstile, a "PUBLIC TRANSIT — COMBINED COMPLEX" sign, and a visible vertical shaft.
- **Surface exit:** (60, 90, −180) on the SubTropolis plateau. From here, players can navigate to the city in the broader valley.
- **Tag:** [X] in both master plans and the combined-complex report.

### 6.5 Cross-references

- The **combined-complex report** (separate document) will describe the full layout of the three builds, the service tunnel's full path, and the public shaft's full path.
- The **map-integration report** (separate document) will describe how the three builds are placed on the shared map.
- This site plan assumes those documents will reference the SubTropolis terminus coordinates listed above.

## 8. Materials & Block Palette (Site-Level)

This is a *site-level* palette. The Architectural Designer will refine it building-by-building.

### 8.1 Exterior limestone (the hill, the ravine walls)

- **Primary:** smooth stone, calcite, polished calcite — for the cream/beige Bethany Falls limestone look. [D]
- **Secondary:** diorite, tuff — for the darker veining and the rougher patches. [D]
- **Tertiary:** deepslate — for occasional veins (used sparingly). [D]
- **Strata:** horizontal bands should be visible, achieved by alternating smooth stone and calcite in 1–2 block layers. [D]

### 8.2 Painted columns (the pillar grid)

- **Primary:** quartz block or calcite — the lighter, more "limestone" feel. [D]
- **Painted white:** white concrete, white terracotta, or white wool — applied to the pillar surfaces to convey the painted-white SubTropolis pillar look. [D]
- **Stenciled numbers:** black wool or black concrete powder — for pillar numbers. [D]

### 8.3 Exposed rock walls (unfinished chambers, ghost mine)

- **Rough-hewn:** stone, cobblestone, mossy cobblestone — for the un-painted, raw limestone of the unfinished zones. [D]
- **Drill marks:** use stairs or slabs to suggest tool scoring. [I inferred]

### 8.4 Tenant floors

- **Newer sections:** polished andesite — for the polished concrete look. [D]
- **Older sections:** stone or gravel — for the rougher concrete/limestone floor. [D]
- **Painted lane lines:** black wool or black concrete — on light gray. [D]
- **Parking-bay markings:** yellow wool — for the tenant fit-out zones. [D]

### 8.5 Painted utility lines

- **Red wool** — fire suppression. [D]
- **Blue wool** — water. [D]
- **Yellow wool** — comms. [D]
- **Green wool** — data. [D]
- These run along the walls and ceiling of the developed corridors, painted directly on the rock. [D]

### 8.6 Surface above the mine

- **Grass, dirt, path blocks** — for the surface plateau. [D]
- **Gravel, coarse dirt** — for the parking lot. [D]
- **Oak / dark oak leaves, vines** — for the tree-covered bluff. [D]

## 11. Conflicts and Tradeoffs (Acknowledged)

- **1:100 floor compression is severe.** The build is ~1% of the real facility. A purist who knows the real SubTropolis will notice that the build is a representative slice, not a literal replica. The Realist accepted this in exchange for a buildable, visitable build. (Per Topic 4, "Tensions and Tradeoffs Acknowledged.")
- **The 14 M sq ft "developed" number is less prominent than the 55 M "mined-out" number.** The 55 M is on the marquee; the 14 M is in the leasing office. This is honest (per Topic 3 decision) but means a player who knows the industry will notice the marketing number being the headline.
- **The service tunnel and public shaft are project fictions, not in the documented SubTropolis history.** They are clearly tagged [X] but a purist will notice. The trade is interoperability with Cheyenne Mountain and the combined-complex project.
- **The Worlds of Fun amusement park is reduced to a single silhouette.** The real SubTropolis has Worlds of Fun directly above. Showing the full park would dilute the "underground" focus.
- **The Hunt/Chiefs reference is a single plaque.** A sports franchise is a documented part of the Hunt legacy, but the build does not have a sports-themed "fan zone."
- **The Ford 25-acre vehicle-storage lease is not a named exhibit.** The Ford lease is documented history but does not get a named build; the space goes to current marquee tenants.
