# References — Houston Tunnel System Research

Numbered list of sources used in `research-report.md`. Format: `[#] Title — Publisher — URL — what it covers`.

---

## Primary / official sources

[1] **Downtown Tunnels** — Downtown Houston District (formerly Central Houston, Inc.) — https://downtownhouston.org/experience/downtown-tunnels — The official tunnel directory: 20 feet below grade, six miles long, 95 city blocks, only Wells Fargo Plaza and McKinney Garage offer direct street access, weekday hours, current tenant listings by tunnel segment (Downtown Tunnel Loop, W. Walker Tunnel, N. Louisiana Tunnel, N. Travis Tunnel, Lamar Tunnel, E. McKinney Tunnel). The authoritative public source for current operations and the 2025 tenant list.

[2] **Above and Below Downtown Houston Map** (Downtown Field Guide & Tunnel Map, Fall 2025) — Downtown Houston District — https://ctycms.com/tx-houston/docs/fieldguide-2025-fall-091025.pdf — Official printable PDF map; the system's only system-wide wayfinding product.

[3] **Public Safety** — Downtown Houston District — https://downtownhouston.org/about/programs/public-safety — Confirms HDMD funds the Downtown Public Safety Guides (28 unarmed guides, white shirts and blue hats, 7 a.m.–11 p.m. 7 days/week), off-duty HPD officers on golf carts, and SEAL Private Security (armed, 24/7). Patrols the surface, not the tunnels.

## Wikipedia (well-cited, Start-class)

[4] **Houston tunnel system** — Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_tunnel_system — Comprehensive article: 95 blocks, 6 miles, 20 feet below grade, building list, discontinuous portions (Harris County, St. Joseph), flooding section. Includes the authoritative count of connected buildings and the explicit disclaimer that the Harris County tunnel and the St. Joseph Medical Center skywalk are not connected to the main system.

[5] **SubTropolis** — Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SubTropolis — 14,000,000 sq ft limestone mine, 65–70°F year-round, 1500 daily workers, 50+ tenants. Used for the comparison section in the report.

## Press / journalism

[6] **Downtown Houston tunnel system: History, hours and more** — Shaniece Holmes-Brown, *Houston Chronicle*, April 12, 2023 — https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/houston-texas/trending/article/downtown-houston-tunnel-system-history-underground-17877560.php — Sandra Lord ("the Tunnel Lady") interview; Ross Sterling / Wyatt C. Henderick origin; Will Horwitz's Uptown Center Project; Horwitz's death 1941; Texan Theater close September 8, 1953; Gerald D. Hines 27-building era; lunchtime economy; Ron Gongora (Red's Barbershop) on the slow days.

[7] **Houston's Downtown Tunnel System** — City Cast Houston, September 12, 2023 (updated September 30, 2025) — https://houston.citycast.fm/history-archive/houston-s-downtown-tunnel-system — Short, reliable: 20 feet below grade, 6 miles, 1930s origin, Gerald D. Hines era, current restaurant tenants. Cites Houston Chronicle and the Above-and-Below map (2016 edition).

[8] **Houston: Subterranean Treasures** — J. Phillips, *Reason* magazine, June 1988 — https://reason.com/1988/06/01/subterranean-treasures/ — Key historical source: first tunnel 1931 (Will Horwitz theaters), 1947 (department store to parking garage), 1951 (first office-to-office tunnel), 2.5 miles by mid-1970s, the "sterile walkway" → "major retail area" transition. Note: full article is paywalled in places; key facts are confirmed by other sources.

[9] **Downtown Houston Tunnel System** — Atlas Obscura — https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/houston-tunnel-system-texas — Cites architectural historian **Stephen Fox** on the 1956 Bank of the Southwest / 1010 Garage / Mellie Esperson Building connection as the origin of the "system" idea. Confirms 95 blocks, 20+ commercial buildings, weekday business hours, largest in the U.S.

[10] **Houston's Downtown Tunnel System: The Underground Guide** — HTXTrip — https://htxtrip.com/blog/downtown-tunnel-system-guide.html — Confirms 6 miles, 95 blocks, 20 feet below grade, weekday business hours; quotes "security is generally relaxed during business hours"; lists access through major office towers and hotels (Lancaster, Hyatt Regency, Marriott Marquis, Hilton Americas).

[11] **One of Houston's Best Kept Secrets: Downtown Tunnels** — Houston Public Media, April 12, 2007 — https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/2007/04/12/6934/one-of-houstons-best-kept-secrets-downtown-tunnels-2/ — Earliest public-radio piece in this research; quotes HDMD's Greg Eury on the "utilitarian function" of the original tunnels (parking-to-basement) and the addition of retail.

[12] **Houston Downtown tunnels: The complete guide to the underground…** — Casiola (vacation-rental blog) — https://www.casiola.com/blog/houston-downtown-tunnels/ — Useful for the access-point list (Wells Fargo, Pennzoil, One Allen Center, McKinney Garage) and the visitor-faq (lunch hours, hotel access, weekday hours, free to walk).

[13] **Here's Your Houston Underground Tunnels Guide** — Secret Houston — https://secrethouston.com/houston-underground-tunnels/ — Confirms 95 blocks, 6 miles, 20 feet below grade, 80+ access points, 6 a.m.–6 p.m. weekdays, two direct street-level entries (Wells Fargo, McKinney).

[14] **365 Things in Houston: History of the Downtown Tunnel System** — 365thingsinhouston.com — https://365thingsinhouston.com/downtown-tunnels-underground-houston/ — Cites Sandra Lord and Discover Houston Tours; Rockefeller Center inspiration; Horwitz's penny arcade.

[15] **History of the Downtown Tunnel System** — Discover Houston Tours (Sandra Lord) — https://discoverhoustontours.com/?page_id=214 — 1935 Will Horwitz project under Capitol between Travis and Milam; included shops, restaurants, penny arcade, German wine tavern; under what is now JPMorgan Chase Tower; Rockefeller Center inspiration.

[16] **Getting to Know Houston's Downtown Tunnels** — AMLI Residential — https://www.amli.com/blog/getting-know-houstons-downtown-tunnels — Notes "seven-mile" length (outlier vs. consensus "six miles"); clarifies depth is "between one and three stories below street level, at an average of about 20 feet underground"; 80+ access points; weekday 6 a.m.–6 p.m.

[17] **Houston's Underground Tunnels: A Hidden City Below** — Nanny Bag — https://www.nannybag.com/en/guides/houston/houstons-underground-tunnels-a-hidden-city-below — Visitor summary; 6 miles, 95 blocks, 20 feet below grade, weekday hours.

[18] **Houston Public Safety: Public Safety Guides** — YouTube, James Kennedy (HDMD Public Safety Manager), 2023 — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6ASwwtOhY_w — 28 DPSGs, 7 a.m.–11 p.m. 7 days/week, white shirts / blue hats; off-duty HPD on golf carts; 713-223-2003 contact number.

## Real estate and leasing

[19] **801 Louisiana St, Houston, TX 77002 — Office/Retail for Lease** — LoopNet — https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/801-Louisiana-St-Houston-TX/14546966/ — Documents tunnel-level "Basement, Ste T120" (1,169 SF) and "Basement, Ste T140" (6,087 SF) at $16.50/SF/yr; confirms tunnel space is a real, listed commercial asset class with NNN lease structure.

[20] **801 Louisiana Street, Houston, TX 77002** — CommercialCafe — https://www.commercialcafe.com/commercial-property/us/tx/houston/801-louisiana-street/ — Average $27.69/SF/yr; tunnel space listed at $13.37–$42.00/SF/yr; building is 105,145 SF, 8 floors.

[21] **Texas McKinney Place Garage — Retail for Lease** — Crexi — https://www.crexi.com/lease/properties/957769/texas-mckinney-place-garage — 928 Main St, 1,077–3,577 SF tunnel-level suites at $14–$16/SF/yr, listed as "Tunnel Level" and "Tunnel Level, Professional Services." Confirms the McKinney Garage as a retail center in its own right.

## Real estate and management

[22] **Cameron Management — Houston, Texas** — http://cameronmanagement.com/ — Confirms Cameron Management as the property management / leasing / engineering firm for the **Esperson buildings (808 Travis)** and **1001 McKinney** — both major tunnel-connected office buildings. The most-confirmed specific management company in the public record.

## Comparators (sister project / context)

[23] **SubTropolis — ULI Case Studies** — Urban Land Institute — https://casestudies.uli.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/C028009.pdf — Confirms SubTropolis temperatures 65–72°F; 50%+ utility savings; ~1,500 daily workers.

[24] **The Story of SubTropolis & KC's Limestone Caves** — KC Yesterday — https://kcyesterday.com/articles/subtropolis — SubTropolis history: opened 1960s, 270-million-year-old limestone, 55 million sq ft, 65–70°F, US Postal Service, National Archives, film storage.

[25] **SubTropolis: A Subterranean Development** — Area Development — https://www.areadevelopment.com/stateResources/missouri/SubTropolis-subterranean-development-Kansas-City-1522121.shtml — 50–70% lower occupancy costs; Dick Ringer of Hunt Midwest Real Estate Development on climate and pricing.

[26] **SubTropolis Underground Warehousing Facility, Kansas City** — Transport Geography — https://transportgeography.org/contents/applications/cold-chain-logistics/subtropolis-warehouse-kansas-city/ — Confirms 18–21°C year-round, 50%+ utility savings vs. surface, free trade zone.

*Source quality is generally high for the operational facts (hours, block count, length, depth, access points) and is lower for the visual / architectural details (no public source publishes a definitive cross-section drawing).
