## Geology sources

[42] **Pikes Peak Granite, Geolex Unit References** — USGS National Geologic Map Database — https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/Geolex/UnitRefs/PikesPeakRefs_9626.html — Authoritative USGS source for Pikes Peak Granite age (1,000–1,100 Ma, Precambrian Y), composition (coarse-grained, pink microcline feldspar), and batholith extent.

[43] **General Geology and Petrology of the Precambrian Rocks of the Southern Front Range** — USGS Professional Paper 0608B — https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0608b/report.pdf — Three Precambrian igneous episodes (1.7, 1.46, 1.04 Ga); the final episode at 1.04 Ga produced the Pikes Peak Granite.

[44] **Rock Units of the Precambrian Basement in Colorado** — USGS Professional Paper 1321A — https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1321a/report.pdf — Source for the anorogenic, epizonal, composite nature of the Pikes Peak batholith; A-type granite classification.

[45] **Geology — Friends of Cheyenne Cañon** — https://www.cheyennecanon.org/geology — Plain-English summary of Pikes Peak Granite weathering, jointing, and uplift. Source for the 65-Mya Laramide uplift and 17-Mya re-uplift.

[46] **Geologic Map of Cheyenne Mountain** — Scribd-hosted USGS map (1970s–1990s) — https://www.scribd.com/document/463160268/Cheyenne-Mt — Ypp = Pikes Peak Granite (Middle Proterozoic), resistant red medium- to coarse-grained granite intrusions. Source for 1.08–1.02 Ga age estimate.

[47] **Cheyenne Mountain Wikipedia article** — Geology section — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne_Mountain — 1,524 m of magma emplacement under surface; granite from solidification; 17 Mya uplift to 9,565 ft. Pikes Peak Granite is "pink and pale orange."

## Note on duplication

Some URLs appear in multiple search results; the authoritative primary or first-citing source is listed first. The Wikipedia articles ([7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]) carry the most weight for cross-referenced facts; the magazine/journalism sources ([21]–[41]) supply the most quotable material; the government primary sources ([1]–[5], [13]–[19]) are the bedrock of factual claims.

Where I have not been able to verify a fact at a primary source (e.g., specific current staff numbers, internal layouts, computer system details), I have flagged the claim as low-confidence in §12 of the report.
