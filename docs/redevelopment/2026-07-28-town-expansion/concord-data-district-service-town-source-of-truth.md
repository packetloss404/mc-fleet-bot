# Concord Data-District Service Town

**Status:** binding design brief; final coordinates require the shared district
terrain/collision survey  
**Build scope:** complete town in the Town Expansion R1 release  
**Adult-content boundary:** all adult venues are represented through
architecture, circulation, color, furniture, stages, and access zoning; no
explicit imagery or figures  
**Prepared:** 2026-07-28 UTC

## Design decision

Concord is the small town travelers do not expect to find beyond the Iowa
data-center district. Its road face looks practical and ordinary: gas station,
post office, bar, motel sign, trees, and a compact two-street grid. One block
behind that frontage is a polished adults-only night court. The court is not an
accidental back lot: a 25-room motel and a smaller opulent theater face each
other across a shared pedestrianized parking court, with a separate dance/strip
club on the short side and reserved entertainment frontage at the rear.

This dual identity makes the town discoverable without dumping every use on the
approach road. Buildings form street walls and visible corners. Parking,
delivery, pedestrian, performer, and utility movements each have a legible
route.

Concord's fictional economy and civic identity are intentionally purpose-built
around consensual adult nightlife for the neighboring data-center construction
and operations workforce. The town treats that as the workers' characteristic
off-shift hobby. Shift-change arrivals, late-night food, hospitality, worker
lounges, the shared adults-only court, discreet circulation, and future
entertainment growth therefore drive the plan rather than appearing as an
afterthought. The build communicates that identity without explicit content.

## Research basis

EPA's small-town smart-growth material recommends focusing development in a
walkable town center, supporting existing or planned infrastructure, protecting
the rural landscape, and deciding clearly where growth should and should not
go. Concord translates that into two compact blocks, a defined future-growth
edge, and preserved landscape between town and data campuses instead of loose
roadside scatter.

Sources:

- [EPA — Smart Growth in Small Towns and Rural Communities](https://www.epa.gov/smartgrowth/smart-growth-small-towns-and-rural-communities)
- [EPA — Putting Smart Growth to Work in Rural Communities](https://www.epa.gov/smartgrowth/putting-smart-growth-work-rural-communities)
- [FHWA — Small Town and Rural Multimodal Networks resources](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pgc/index.cfm?ddisc=121&dsub=1475)

The U.S. Access Board states that a site's accessible route should connect
parking, loading, streets/sidewalks, and accessible entrances, and that parking
access aisles must connect directly to those routes. Its transient-lodging
standards inform the motel's same-quality accessible-room analogues and
continuous circulation. This Minecraft build does not claim legal compliance.

Sources:

- [U.S. Access Board — Accessible Routes](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-accessible-routes/)
- [U.S. Access Board — Parking](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-5-parking/)
- [U.S. Access Board — ADA Standards and transient lodging](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/)

USPS describes small community post offices as local service institutions and
documents contract/community post-office models. That supports a compact,
frontage-facing postal counter and sorting room rather than an oversized civic
monument.

Sources:

- [USPS — Stations and Branches history](https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/pdf/stations-branches.pdf)
- [USPS — Smallest Post Office](https://facts.usps.com/smallest-post-office/)

## Street and block plan

1. **Concord Road / ordinary frontage**
   - two travel lanes with a planted pedestrian edge;
   - gas station and convenience store at the first corner;
   - compact post office on the town-square corner;
   - neighborhood bar opposite the post office;
   - motel arrival/office visible at the night-court turn;
   - marked worker-shuttle stop and shift-change waiting lounge;
   - no blank building backs facing the road.
2. **Night Court**
   - shared parking court with a protected center walk and planted islands;
   - motel on one long side, Gilded Raven Annex theater directly opposite;
   - separate dance/strip-club venue on the short side;
   - age-control entries face the court; deliveries and performers use the rear
     service lane;
   - architecture is opulent but non-explicit.
3. **Service Lane**
   - motel housekeeping/loading, theater scenery/loading, club deliveries,
     waste/service yards, and utility access;
   - screened from the shared court but not hidden as a maze.
4. **Future Entertainment Row**
   - two graded, landscaped parcels with completed sidewalk/utility stubs;
   - no temporary clutter or parking that would have to be demolished;
   - the street terminates in a planted turning court that can extend later.

## Building programs

### Concord Fuel & General

- eight covered fueling positions represented by four double-sided islands;
- convenience store with counter, grocery wall, coolers, small hot-food area,
  staff room, storage, and toilets;
- separate service apron and delivery door;
- visible canopy, price-tower analogue, safe pedestrian path, and protected
  landscape buffer from the town sidewalk.

### Concord Community Post

- small public lobby and counter;
- PO-box wall;
- secure-looking but non-tactical sorting room;
- carrier worktables, parcel room, staff office/break room, and rear loading
  door;
- civic clock/sign frame and a bench plaza at the front corner.

### The Last Relay bar

- neighborhood bar, roughly 40 seats;
- horseshoe counter, booths, small stage, kitchen, cold/storage room, staff
  room, and toilets;
- front porch facing Concord Road and a quieter rear garden;
- no connection into the adult theater's performer routes.

### Concord Motor Lodge — exactly 25 rooms

- two-story courtyard motel;
- 13 guest rooms at ground level and 12 at upper level;
- two ground-floor same-quality mobility-feature analogues nearest the lobby;
- every room receives a sleeping zone, washroom, wardrobe, desk/seating, and
  exterior window;
- lobby/office, two-room manager office and lounge, breakfast nook, laundry,
  linen/housekeeping on both levels, staff room, vending alcove, toilets, and
  enclosed broad stair/lift cores at opposite ends;
- balconies and room doors face the shared night court; service rooms face the
  rear lane;
- the motel sign and public lobby are visible from Concord Road without making
  the adult court explicit from the highway.

### Gilded Raven Annex — smaller adult theater

- 72-seat main room with a real stage, proscenium, two balcony tiers, and
  ceiling centerpiece;
- open bowl-edge circulation rather than doors into confusing blind corridors;
- three five-seat private salon theaters and two single-guest performance
  salons, all non-graphic;
- court bar, supper room, two small restaurant rooms, coat/check-in, and
  age-control lobby;
- dressing rooms, green room, wardrobe, rehearsal room, scenery store, loading
  door, staff break room, and performer-only rear route;
- one concealed public discovery passage from the town bar garden to the
  theater court, with an ordinary signed route also present;
- one owner/management room and a short local service passage only. This mini
  theater does **not** connect to the Worker Town owner tunnel or Ravensgate.

### Concord Night House — dance/strip-club venue

- separate lobby and age-control threshold;
- one central non-graphic dance/performance stage with believable audience
  sightlines;
- lounge booths, bar, small kitchen, coat check, toilets, two dressing rooms,
  green room, wardrobe, office/count room, storage, and rear loading;
- no explicit figures, images, or text.

## Terrain, parking, and landscape

- Fit the two-block plan to surveyed stepped benches. Use short planted slopes
  between street levels; do not place the town on one giant artificial slab.
- The motel/theater joint lot is a real shared court: marked circulation around
  the perimeter, planted center walk, drop-off, protected accessible-route
  analogues, and a direct link to every public entrance.
- Gas-station circulation stays outside the night-court pedestrian core.
- If the selected parcel includes useful drainage, make it a small planted
  town pond/detention garden at the future-entertainment edge. Drain only an
  isolated nuisance pocket after the district fluid census.
- Preserve a green buffer between the town and each power yard or transmission
  corridor.

## Release gates

- gas station, post office, bar, motel, theater, and club are fully enclosed and
  walkable;
- motel room count is exactly 25, with 13 ground and 12 upper rooms;
- motel and theater face the same shared court;
- main theater has exactly 72 seats, three five-seat salons, and two
  single-guest salons;
- ordinary/public, service, performer, and management routes are distinct;
- no mini-theater route connects to Ravensgate, the Worker Town owner corridor,
  or an offline portal room;
- two future entertainment parcels and an extendable street/utility stub remain;
- grading uses terrain benches and planted transitions, not a flat mega-pad;
- town/power separation and parking/pedestrian routes are legible;
- no active portal, lava, bubble-column, or introduced aquatic state;
- protected block-entity and live-entity gates pass;
- exact rollback covers every town target.
