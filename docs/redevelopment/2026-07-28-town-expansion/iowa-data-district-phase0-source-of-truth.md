# Iowa Data District — Full Build and Shared Worker Commons

**Status:** binding full-build design and release brief; physical/as-built status
must be taken from the final guarded release and post-release verification  
**Supersedes:** the planned/unbuilt Phase 0 envelope-only program in every
respect  
**Authorized scope:** twelve Meta-inspired halls, six Google-inspired halls,
two LightEdge Solutions EdgeBCC-inspired buildings, their support facilities,
roads, utilities, district power, complete fictional walkable interiors, and a
shared naturalized worker commons  
**Prepared:** 2026-07-28 UTC

The owner has authorized all three additional Iowa-inspired precincts for full
construction in this release. They are no longer construction outlines for a
future session. The shared worker commons is also a full-build target: a large
drainage-informed pond, naturalized green space, a continuous walk/bike loop
beginning in Concord, shelter houses, small park amenities, and an eighteen-hole
disc-golf course.

This document deliberately separates three kinds of truth:

1. **Public precedent** is limited to facts and themes published by Meta,
   Google, LightEdge Solutions, government agencies, and the PDGA.
2. **Project design** is an original fictional Minecraft interpretation.
   Building positions, rooms, rack patterns, utilities, roads, trails, power
   infrastructure, landscape, and disc-golf routing do not represent real
   facilities.
3. **As-built truth** exists only after the guarded operation package is
   committed, the immutable post-release snapshot passes QA, every object is
   imported into the world catalog, and its matched after evidence is accepted.

The companion machine schedule is
`iowa-data-district-full-build-coordinate-schedule.json`. Where that file and
this narrative differ, the final independently audited generator report and
post-release snapshot control physical truth.

## 1. Public-source research boundary

### 1.1 Meta Altoona precedent

Meta stated in May 2021 that two new Altoona buildings would bring the campus
to ten buildings. Its December 2021 announcement added two more buildings and
described more than five million square feet on completion. That supports a
**twelve-building public planning precedent**. Meta also publicly describes
energy efficiency, renewable-energy matching, water stewardship, construction
waste diversion, and LEED certification across its owned data-center program.

Sources:

- [Meta — May 2021 Altoona expansion](https://datacenters.atmeta.com/2021/05/we-are-expanding-the-facebook-altoona-data-center/)
- [Meta — December 2021 Altoona expansion](https://datacenters.atmeta.com/2021/12/meta-announces-new-expansion-to-altoona-campus-new-hardhat-in-hand-graduates/)
- [Meta — data-center sustainability](https://sustainability.atmeta.com/data-centers/)

Project translation: build twelve original compact Minecraft halls in three
four-hall neighborhoods. Repeat a coherent architectural rhythm while giving
each neighborhood a distinct color band, courtyard planting, and wayfinding
identity. The project does not copy any real Altoona floor plan, perimeter,
network, power entry, or access-control design.

### 1.2 Google Council Bluffs precedent

Google's official Iowa page dates its Council Bluffs investment to 2007 and
records multiple later expansions. It does not publish a simple authoritative
current building count. The project count of **six halls** is therefore an
explicit fictional design choice, not a claim about Council Bluffs. Google's
public gallery identifies such general program elements as a hub with offices,
control and security rooms, cafeteria, gym, and yoga studio; a modular global
central utilities building, or gCUB; outdoor staff seating; electrical yards;
and visible server aisles.

Sources:

- [Google Data Centers — Iowa](https://datacenters.google/locations/iowa/)
- [Google Data Centers — public photo gallery](https://www.datacenters.google/discover-more/photo-gallery/)

Project translation: build six original Minecraft halls in three paired courts,
two fictional modular utility buildings, a visitor/operations hub, and a
repair/staging building. The design may evoke Google's published use of color,
staff commons, and modular support buildings. It must not reproduce real
security, network, plant, or access geometry.

### 1.3 LightEdge Solutions EdgeBCC precedent

The correct name is **EdgeBCC**, not EdgeConneX. LightEdge's 2011 public release
described an Altoona data-center expansion for colocation and private cage
space and stated that an additional property option could support two similar
facilities with four data-center spaces per facility.

Source:
[LightEdge Solutions — EdgeBCC facility expansion](https://lightedge.com/lightedge-solutions-edgebcc-facility-raises-16m-data-center-expansion/)

Project translation: build two original Minecraft data-center/business-
continuity buildings. Each building has four legible fictional quadrants,
walkable colo aisles, a shared central circulation cross, and support spaces.
The two-building/four-quadrant composition is a public-inspired architectural
story, not an audit or replica of the real site.

### 1.4 Water, landscape, path, and recreation precedent

Iowa DNR explains that development adds impervious area and runoff and that
stormwater planning should combine water-quality, infiltration, channel
protection, and conventional drainage practices. Its stormwater manual includes
wet ponds, constructed wetlands, swales, soil restoration, native landscaping,
and natural-resource inventory. That guidance supports preserving surveyed
drainage and shaping a pond with planted transition zones instead of flattening
the district into one grass slab.

Sources:

- [Iowa DNR — stormwater overview](https://www.iowadnr.gov/environmental-protection/water-quality/watershed-improvement/stormwater)
- [Iowa DNR — Iowa Stormwater Management Manual](https://www.iowadnr.gov/environmental-protection/water-quality/stormwater-program/iswmm-manual)
- [Iowa DNR — native landscaping guidance](https://www.iowadnr.gov/media/4883/download?inline=)

FHWA describes shared-use paths as low-stress, two-way facilities separated
from motor traffic and serving people walking, cycling, rolling, jogging, and
using mobility devices. It emphasizes firm and stable surfaces, accessible
grades, and careful treatment where paths meet roads. The Access Board requires
continuous unobstructed connections among arrival points, trailheads, and
provided accessible features in the outdoor contexts covered by its guidance.

Sources:

- [FHWA — greenways and shared-use paths](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/pedbike/05085/chapt19.cfm)
- [U.S. Access Board — outdoor developed areas](https://www.access-board.gov/aba/guides/chapter-10-outdoor/)
- [U.S. Access Board — accessible trails and shared-use paths](https://www.access-board.gov/webinars/2021/08/05/accessible-pedestrian-trails-and-shared-use-paths/)

The PDGA's public course-development goals call for visibility of players and
other users, a balanced range of hole lengths and shot shapes, effective use of
elevation and foliage, and erosion protection. The PDGA recommends experienced
professional design for real courses. This Minecraft course is a fictional
recreational model and is not PDGA-certified.

Sources:

## 2. District master plan

### 2.1 Spatial hierarchy

The district must read in this order while approaching from Concord:

1. the Concord trailhead and worker-arrival porch;
2. a naturalized transition from town to campus;
3. the shared pond and worker commons as the district's visual center;
4. the three distinct campus identities around the commons;
5. service roads and fenced power infrastructure beyond the public realm; and
6. a protected utility/future-growth edge.

The pond, trees, paths, shelter roofs, campus courts, and changing terrain must
break the long views. A player should never see an uninterrupted field of flat
grass filled only with rectangular buildings.

### 2.2 Full-build counts

| Precinct | Data buildings | Support buildings | Required interior identity |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Meta Altoona-inspired | 12 | 2 or more shared support objects | repeating hall spine, walkable rack aisles, electrical/mechanical gallery, staff room, color-coded four-hall neighborhood |
| Google Iowa-inspired | 6 | 4 | paired hall courts, visible aisle rhythm, two modular utility buildings, visitor/operations hub, repair/staging |
| LightEdge EdgeBCC-inspired | 2 | 2 | four quadrants per building, colo/cage rhythm, central cross-corridor, recovery/admin and service support |
| Shared district | 0 | pond, shelters, trailhead, park, power and maintenance objects | public worker commons, continuous nonmotorized loop, landscape and recreation |

Required physical count: **twenty completed walkable data buildings**. A low
outline, empty pad, construction billboard, or future envelope does not count.

### 2.3 Common interior minimum

Every data building must include, at Minecraft scale:

- an identifiable staffed entrance or service vestibule;
- a continuous walkable route from a door to the far end and back;
- individually legible rack or cage rows with clear aisles;
- visible overhead or edge utility language;
- a cooling/electrical/service zone that is visually distinct from white space;
- at least one staff support or maintenance room;
- emergency/secondary egress language where the footprint permits it;
- interior lighting sufficient for screenshots without night-vision effects;
- no one-block pinch point on the primary route;
- no decorative block occupying the player's head or foot volume; and
- two matched camera candidates: exterior identity and interior depth.

These are presentation rules, not a real operational specification. No hall may
be advertised as a reproduction of a company's real infrastructure.

## 3. Precinct programs

### 3.1 Meta — twelve-hall field

The three four-hall groups are `META-N1`, `META-N2`, and `META-N3`. Each group
uses the same basic building family, then changes color band, planting palette,
and courtyard furniture. The repetition should make the campus comprehensible;
the differences should keep it from looking copied and pasted.

Each hall must contain:

- a front vestibule and hall marker;
- a long primary aisle;
- paired rack fields separated by transverse clear paths;
- one visible mechanical/electrical gallery;
- a compact tool/store room;
- a two-zone staff break and briefing nook; and
- an independently visible rear/service exit.

The shared Meta support program includes an operations/training pavilion,
materials-recovery/service yard, shaded courtyard tables, bicycle parking, and
a direct path gate into the worker commons.

### 3.2 Google — six-hall paired courts

The three pairs are `GOOGLE-P1`, `GOOGLE-P2`, and `GOOGLE-P3`. Each pair faces a
small planted court and shares a support connection without becoming one giant
blank façade.

Each hall must contain:

- a colored arrival threshold;
- a main aisle with multiple visible rack rows;
- a front-of-room cooling/utility analogue;
- a narrow maintenance gallery;
- a staff workroom and recovery nook; and
- a clear second way out.

The public-inspired but fictional support objects are:

- two modular utility buildings;
- one visitor/operations hub with reception, control-room analogue, offices,
  cafeteria, gym/yoga room, lockers, and shaded outdoor seating; and
- one repair/staging shop with truck court, parts racks, work bays, and a staff
  desk.

The visitor hub is the public connection to the shared worker commons. No path
through a data hall is required to reach the pond or park.

### 3.3 LightEdge Solutions EdgeBCC — two four-quadrant buildings

`EDGEBCC-A` and `EDGEBCC-B` are broader than the hyperscale halls. Each has:

- four labeled colo/data quadrants;
- a central north-south/east-west circulation cross;
- walkable rack or cage rows in every quadrant;
- a NOC/support room analogue;
- customer staging and crash-work area;
- utility/support rooms at the service edge; and
- a clearly separate main entrance and service entrance.

The shared recovery/admin building contains reception, training, conference,
open work tables, kitchen, dining/break area, quiet room, showers/lockers, and
two small recovery-work suites. A separate service court holds repair and
delivery details.

## 4. Shared worker commons

### 4.1 The pond is infrastructure and public space

The central pond is formed around the surveyed drainage/water feature, not
dropped onto an arbitrary flat pad. The final generator must record every
pre-existing fluid column and its disposition. The full-build landscape may
expand the water body, but it must do so through a reviewed, bounded shoreline
package with exact rollback.

Required pond composition:

- an irregular shoreline with at least three visible concave bays;
- a planted shallow/wetland shelf represented with non-obstructing material;
- at least two high-bank overlooks and one low-bank viewing deck;
- a maintenance forebay/weir expression kept outside the main public lawn;
- a bridge or boardwalk only where it improves route continuity;
- a no-throw shoreline sector where disc flight could otherwise cross water or
  a busy path;
- dark and light planting bands that read as wet, wet-mesic, mesic, and upland
  zones; and
- enough clear water to be a district landmark from Meta, Google, and
  EdgeBCC.

The pond is a fictional Minecraft stormwater landscape. The release must not
claim real detention volume, water-quality performance, dam engineering, or
regulatory approval.

### 4.2 Naturalized terrain and planting

Landscape is modeled as clustered ecology, not decoration sprinkled on a slab.

- Preserve terrain movement and use shallow terraces only where buildings or
  accessible routes require them.
- Blend every cut/fill edge with two or more planted steps.
- Use asymmetric tree groves, small understory groups, prairie bands, and open
  lawns in deliberate proportions.
- Keep major building entrances and path intersections visible.
- Keep roots/trunks out of required path clear width, doors, utility yards,
  transmission corridors, and disc-golf throw lanes.
- Use taller/denser trees to screen service yards and longer blank elevations.
- Use lower planting at the pond edge and trail crossings.
- Vary grove density so the course offers open, wooded-edge, and tunnel shots
  without blind throws.
- Record every tree cluster as an object group with bounds and a camera
  candidate. Random unreported tree placement fails the object-evidence rule.

No campus landscape passes if an atlas view still reads as a flat grass sheet
with isolated boxes.

### 4.3 Continuous Concord walk/bike loop

The Concord trailhead is the zero point. From it, a continuous off-road route
must reach and loop all three campus precincts, the pond, the park, every
shelter house, and the disc-golf start/finish area before returning to Concord.

Minecraft route standard:

- three clear blocks minimum throughout the compact model;
- firm, visually consistent surface;
- no jumps;
- no ladders;
- headroom of at least three blocks;
- terrain-following grades with broad landings where the route changes level;
- protected separation from motor roads or an unmistakable controlled crossing;
- widened passing/rest nodes at the trailhead, shelters, pond overlooks, and
  campus gates;
- shore-side railing or planted buffer where a direct fall into water is
  possible;
- lighting at intersections and gathering points, with darker habitat edges
  left deliberate; and
- a route graph that passes in both directions with the normal walking client.

This is an accessibility-informed fictional model, not a claim of legal
certification. The post-release route test must record both directions,
obstruction count, jumps, minimum clear width, grade transitions, and every
road crossing.

### 4.4 Trailhead, park, and shelter houses

The Concord trailhead must contain:

- a visible gateway/map object;
- bicycle racks;
- drinking fountain analogue;
- repair stand;
- seating with adjacent companion spaces;
- waste/recycling point;
- a roofed information kiosk;
- lighting and a district-emergency marker; and
- direct, step-free connection to Concord's public path.

Provide at least four shelter houses:

1. Concord Trailhead Shelter — information, rest, and bike-repair focus;
2. Pond Commons Shelter — tables, grills, water view, and broad gathering lawn;
3. North Prairie Shelter — quieter staff lunch and bird/prairie overlook; and
4. EdgeBCC Grove Shelter — small-team outdoor work and recovery focus.

At each shelter, the primary route reaches a level floor; seating/table groups
leave a clear companion position; grills or fire features are offset from roof
supports and vegetation; and disc fairways do not cross the occupied floor.

The park program also includes an open event lawn, a small nature-play area, a
fitness/stretch node, two pond overlooks, benches at sensible intervals, and
informal outdoor work tables with shade.

## 5. Eighteen-hole disc-golf course

### 5.1 Course identity

The fictional course is **Three Campuses Disc Golf**. It begins and ends beside
the Concord trailhead and uses the worker commons, prairie edges, pond views,
campus groves, and terrain changes. It is a staff amenity shared by all three
campuses, not a separate fenced attraction.

Required composition:

- exactly eighteen numbered tees;
- exactly eighteen numbered baskets;
- exactly eighteen tee signs;
- a start map and finish marker at Concord;
- a balanced mix of straight, left-finishing, right-finishing, uphill,
  downhill, open, wooded-edge, and water-view holes;
- a reviewed par for every hole and published total par;
- explicit tee-to-basket and basket-to-next-tee routing;
- no intended flight over a building, vehicle road, shelter floor, playground,
  occupied terrace, trailhead, power yard, transmission corridor, or blind path
  crossing; and
- no hole accepted solely because its objects fit. The entire flight corridor
  and the walk to the next tee must pass collision and visibility review.

The exact hole schedule, tee/basket positions, pars, centerline waypoints,
camera IDs, and safety fields live in the companion JSON. Course lengths are
Minecraft blocks and are not represented as real-world feet or meters.

### 5.2 Safety and circulation controls

- Tees point away from the busiest commons features.
- Where a fairway approaches the general trail, the basket is placed before the
  conflict and the next-tee route crosses at a visible right angle.
- Pond-edge holes are water-view holes, not forced water carries.
- Trees shape shots but do not create a blind landing zone.
- Tee signs identify hole, par, block length, intended line, next tee, and
  no-throw sector.
- Baskets remain visible from their tees or from a clearly identified dogleg
  turn.
- Each hole has a minimum camera pair: tee view and reverse basket view.
- Final visual QA must be done once with landscape complete, then repeated
  after defects are corrected.

## 6. Roads, utilities, and power

The full campus build retains the substantial shared power system authorized
for Phase 0, but the status changes from reservations to completed fictional
site infrastructure.

Required district power objects:

- one shared switching yard;
- two visually independent transmission corridors;
- one completed substation yard for each of Meta, Google, and EdgeBCC;
- transformer-bank and buswork analogues with maintenance clearances;
- protected service/firebreak lanes;
- an energy-operations building;
- future storage/operations pads that remain visibly reserved;
- planted screening from public paths without hiding every landmark; and
- continuing utility capacity at the district edge.

Required campus roads:

- exact connection to the existing east campus loop;
- public/spine road continuity past Concord and the commons;
- a complete service loop or service court for each campus;
- separated public and service approaches at Google;
- legible delivery access for all support buildings;
- no road that stops in grass without a defined turnaround, gate, or future
  stub; and
- no path crossing at a truck bay, transformer gate, or blind bend.

All equipment, routes, capacities, and control systems are fictional visual
analogues. No real voltage, protection scheme, line route, operational detail,
or security arrangement is asserted.

Publication must not infer a relationship merely because an image is nearby.
