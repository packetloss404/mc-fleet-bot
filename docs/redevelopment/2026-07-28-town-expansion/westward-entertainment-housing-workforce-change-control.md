## 1. Controlling directives

### 1.1 Facing amusement-park pair

Create two researched, facing amusement parks west of Westlight:

- a dry amusement park informed by the operating and spatial lessons of
  Adventureland and Worlds of Fun;
- a water park informed by the operating and spatial lessons of Adventure Bay
  and Oceans of Fun; and
- a shared arrival composition that makes the two gates read as a deliberate
  pair without making them identical.

These references are precedent prompts, not copy instructions. The final
district must be original, scaled to the world, independently named, and
supported by cited research into entry sequences, ride zoning, water
attractions, family zones, service circulation, safety, queues, food,
landscape, parking/transit analogues, and night identity.

### 1.2 Westward approach extension and crater-green integration

Extend the Westlight approach road westward to serve both parks. Coordinate it
with the stadium-island Main Mall, the amusement pier, and the crater-lake green
belt. The result must be one legible destination corridor with a clear street
hierarchy, protected pedestrian crossings, service access, and an unbroken
public green/water relationship.

The extension must not turn the crater lake into a traffic island or sever its
continuous quay/green loop.

### 1.3 Lakeside housing

Create a Hampton-style medium-density lakeside neighborhood integrated with the
crater-lake green belt. “Hampton-style” controls a coherent residential
character—generous porches, gables, restrained coastal materials, planted
setbacks, and a strong public-lakeside address—and does not authorize copying a
specific private residence.

The plan must balance:

- continuous public access to the lake;
- private and semiprivate residential thresholds;
- medium-density blocks rather than isolated estate lots;
- complete street, alley/service, and attached-garage logic;
- views without privatizing the entire shoreline;
- drainage, grade, and safe water edges; and
- direct walking connections to Westlight and the parks.

Every residential garage remains subject to
`REDEV-2026-07-28-CR-ATTACHED-GARAGES-01`: it must be attached to its parent
house, and every large house must have four or six clear attached bays.

### 1.4 Southwest Ravensreach housing

Create two affordable/distressed-housing projects southwest of Ravensreach.
They must connect to one another and each must organize around a useful central
outdoor space. “Distressed” describes the intended economic/program condition;
it is not permission for unsafe routes, inaccessible doors, arbitrary ruin, or
dehumanizing caricature.

The projects require:

- distinct identities within one connected neighborhood;
- complete dwelling, common, service, and address schedules;
- central outdoor spaces with seating, play/community, light, planting, and
  visible entries;
- safe pedestrian links between the two projects and to Ravensreach;
- credible service/refuse/loading access;
- lighting and passive-surveillance sightlines;
- no unexplained leftover gaps; and
- exact building, room, route, and media database relationships.

### 1.5 Staff path and employee lounge

Build a direct staff path from Ravensreach to the rear of the MainStreet main
building and provide an employee lounge at that destination. The route must be
recognizably staff-oriented while remaining safe and legible:

- surveyed origin and exact rear-service destination;
- continuous normal-walk surface and adequate headroom;
- stairs/landings that are comfortable in both directions;
- lighting, signs, crossings, weather-cover cues where appropriate, and no
  accidental public dead ends;
- separation from incompatible loading or secure areas; and
- an employee lounge with seating, food/drink support, lockers/storage,
  wash/support access, and a clear connection to the staff entrance.

## 3. Research and survey packages

### 3.1 Park precedent research

Use authoritative park/operator, planning, engineering, accessibility, or
historic sources. The research paper must:

- compare Adventureland/Adventure Bay and Worlds of Fun/Oceans of Fun as paired
  dry/water-park precedents;
- distinguish transferable planning lessons from branded or protected forms;
- map gate, midway, family, thrill, water, food, service, maintenance,
  emergency, and landscape zones;
- evaluate queue length, pedestrian capacity, ride clearance, sightlines,
  night identity, and phased growth;
- select one original paired-park concept and reject at least two alternatives;
  and
- document all citations and the final design translation.

### 3.2 Westward land and route survey

Survey:

- shoreline, crater-lake basin, westward terrain, grades, water, caves, trees,
  fluids, block entities, inventories, and entities;
- every existing road, bridge, path, parcel, protected object, and view;
- candidate park gates, service edges, emergency returns, and expansion
  reserves;
- candidate housing blocks and lake access;
- exact Ravensreach housing parcels;
- exact staff-path origin/destination and rear MainStreet service geometry; and
- cross-package intersections with the amusement pier, restaurants, boulevard,
  oasis, and current database features.

Outputs must include north-up base maps, grade/water maps, protected-object
overlays, figure-ground diagrams, route/sightline diagrams, and a conflict
register.

## 4. Work packages

### WP-01 — Paired-park master plan

Create an original facing-gate composition with:

- shared ceremonial arrival and orientation space;
- dry-park and water-park entries that are distinct but coordinated;
- clear ticket/security/queue zones;
- guest, staff, service, maintenance, and emergency route systems;
- ride/attraction districts, food, restrooms/support, first-aid, storage, and
  operations;
- a night landmark visible from the approach but not blocking stadium
  sightlines; and
- phased expansion parcels that do not look unfinished.

### WP-02 — Dry amusement park

Minimum planning program:

- gate street or entry plaza;
- family zone;
- major thrill-ride zone;
- at least one coherent coaster station/support/clearance package;
- flat-ride or observation landmark;
- food/event court;
- maintenance/service compound; and
- complete guest and emergency-return loops.

### WP-03 — Water park

Minimum planning program:

- controlled gate and changing/support zone;
- family/splash area;
- pool or basin attraction;
- slide/tower complex with legible supports and runouts;
- shaded/rest areas translated to the world’s material language;
- food/support/first-aid;
- plant/service interpretation; and
- exact contained-water and neighbor-fluid proof.

### WP-04 — Westward approach

Extend the road with:

- surveyed horizontal and vertical alignment;
- consistent carriageway, sidewalks/greenway, lighting, signs, and crossings;
- decision points for the stadium, pier, lake, housing, dry park, and water
  park;
- separated service access where feasible;
- coordinated billboard/view policy; and
- normal-walk tests in every required direction.

### WP-05 — Crater-green-belt integration

Preserve a continuous public green loop while connecting:

- stadium and pier;
- crater lake/quay;
- lakeside housing;
- both park gates; and
- the westward road and transit/parking analogues.

Every road crossing requires an explicit pedestrian treatment. No new parcel
may block the lake loop or consume an unrecorded protected edge.

### LH-01 — Hampton-style lakeside neighborhood

Provide:

- measured block and lot schedule;
- medium-density building types and height/setback controls;
- public waterfront walk and selected view corridors;
- local streets, rear service where appropriate, and attached garages;
- shared greens/courts at useful intervals;
- building/room/address IDs; and
- exterior, street, waterfront, courtyard, and floor-plan evidence.

### RH-01 — Southwest project A

Provide a complete connected housing project centered on a programmed outdoor
space. Define every dwelling building, common room, community/service space,
entrance, street/path address, and operational route.

### RH-02 — Southwest project B

Provide a second, distinct but connected housing project with the same
completeness. Its central outdoor space must complement rather than duplicate
Project A.

### RH-03 — Housing connection and dignity audit

Prove:

- continuous A ↔ B ↔ Ravensreach normal-walk routes;
- visible and safe central-space entries;
- no trapped courts, inaccessible units, forced jumps, or unexplained ruins;
- service access without dominating the central spaces;
- lighting and sightline coverage; and
- a coherent edge where new projects meet existing Ravensreach.

### ST-01 — Staff path

Publish a station/grade/headroom schedule, protected-intersection report,
forward/rollback package, and bidirectional walk report for:

`Ravensreach staff origin ↔ MainStreet rear staff entrance`.

### ST-02 — Employee lounge

Publish a room plan, adjacency/operations schedule, protected-object report,
exact transaction package, interior/exterior captures, and database relation
for the lounge. The lounge may not obstruct existing back-of-house circulation,
inventories, or loading.
